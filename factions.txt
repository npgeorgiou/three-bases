THREE BASES — FACTIONS
The game as it might become: planned factions, faction candidates, card and rule ideas, the design method for new factions, and rejected ideas. The current factions and their cards are in summary.txt section 11.
Goal (AGREED, 11 Oct 2026): 100 cards per faction; spells are 20% to 50% of a faction's cards, depending on its profile.
Work plan (AGREED): step 1, faction candidates from the inspiration library (part 3), discussed at length; step 2, the list of factions to use, then their cards.

THE ROTBORN: decay is transformation, not destruction ("Nothing dies. It becomes something else."). Mold, mushrooms, moss, swollen roots, decaying flesh, and rats (growth, spread, disease); magic of blight, fungal growth, regeneration, decomposition; spore priests, plague beasts, corpse-fungi, rot giants. Twist: their diseases can heal ecosystems and restore dead soil. Leaving remains behind is its main thing (AGREED, 10 Oct 2026): its units leave remains (e.g. Fungal Corpses: 0|0, Immobile, destroyed by any damage, holding dots) that its other cards feed on, sprout from or spread through; what remains do is to be designed. 3. FACTION CANDIDATES (step 1, third version, 11 Oct 2026; Claude's suggestions, under discussion, nothing agreed)

REVIEW NOTES (11 Oct 2026): Part 3 is a list of RAW IDEAS, not a source: check every claim against summary.txt section 11 and the library. Every mechanic names library concept ids in brackets (concepts table; use concept_tree for parents and children), so it can be looked up. They are Claude's mapping of its own wording: lookup handles, to be verified against real cards. They are STARTING POINTS, NOT LIMITS: widen each one through concept_tree (parents and children), cooccur and concept_relations, and add, replace or drop mechanics freely where the library shows something better. Thin entries are expected to grow. This version predates Part 5 and is to be remade in the format of Part 6. Open fixes for the remake are in todo.md.

How this list was built: in the library, a faction is never one mechanic. Each one combines two or three archetypes whose mechanics feed each other (Shadow Isles: sacrifice + graveyard + lifesteal; Duelyst's Magmar: growth + sustain; Lyonar: sustain + fortress; Gwent's Monsters: sacrifice + damage over time + swarm). So every candidate below has one overarching idea, carried by 3-5 mechanics that work together, and covers on its own the four things every Three Bases faction needs: units that hold dots, a way to remove enemies, supply, and cards.
Across the list, every faction gets a reason to use its second and back rows (healers, growers, casters, dot holders from row 2, screened strikers), because in play units pile into the front row.
Terms: defence is one number. Damage lowers it, and restoring brings it back up to what the unit had. A lasting defence loss ("loses 2 defence", as Creeping Rot's "-2 strength") lowers it for good, and restoring can't undo it.
Keyword values and example stats are suggestions; stats follow the supply budget (summary.txt section 9) minus the suggested values. NEW RULE = the current rules can't do it yet. E1-E3 are the existing factions, written as if not designed yet.

E1. TAGMA — the Byzantine army (all-round)
------------------------------------------------------------------
STATUS: VERY MUCH up for BIG redesign. The current cards (summary.txt section 11) are a starting point only; any of them can be changed or dropped. Do not describe Tagma from its current cards: they lean defensive only because of how earlier card design went. Tagma is NOT a defence faction (Kaptain, 11 Oct 2026).
Identity (Kaptain, 11 Oct 2026): an all-round faction, good at everything, flashy at nothing, with the character of a thousand years of East Roman (Byzantine) military, social and spiritual history:
- Adaptable: it changed its army to fit each enemy; mixed troops working together.
- Mercenaries: it hired foreign units (Varangians and others), strong but bought, with their own terms.
- Diplomacy: it won wars without battles: bribes, alliances, turning enemies against each other.
- Logistics: themes, supply trains, fortified roads; armies that arrived fed and on time.
- Strategy: master strategists who wrote manuals (Strategikon, Taktika); plans, stratagems, the right answer prepared in advance.
- Faith: deep spirituality and religiosity; priests, icons and relics with the army. "En touto nika" (in this sign, conquer).
Mechanic directions (Claude's suggestions; the remake picks 3-5 as core, the rest become splash or flavour; library concept ids in brackets):
- Adaptability / combined arms: rewards for mixing different unit roles; cards that choose between modes. [variety_payoff, modal_choice, synergy_payoff, adjacency]
- Mercenaries: strong units bought with supply, carrying terms (upkeep, desertion, a price). [upkeep, drawback, resource_conversion]
- Diplomacy: winning over or pacifying enemy units; bargains. [steal, cant_attack, disable, enemy_benefit]
- Logistics: supply, cheap repositioning, carry-over planning. [resource_gain, round_carryover, movement, rows]
- Strategy: set up now, pay off later; finding the prepared answer (a candidate for the deck-search signature, pending the allocation table). [delayed, tutor, reactions, combo_sequence]
- Faith: blessings, restoring, protection, faith that grows while you hold the lane's base. [heal, protection, aura, buff, territory_trigger]

E2. ASHFANG HORDE — the war host of fury and fire
------------------------------------------------------------------
STATUS: VERY MUCH up for BIG redesign. The current cards (summary.txt section 11) are a starting point only; any of them can be changed or dropped.
Identity: a savage war host of brutes, beasts and fire. Blood is fuel: it hurts itself on purpose to unleash something bigger. Its heart is its huge brutes.
Measured (11 Oct 2026, 168 simulated games): the Horde wins with its big units (Iron Behemoth, Gorehound, Cave Troll); the self-harm payoffs barely mattered and Blood Drum hurt only its own side. Self-harm must feed the big units, not be a tax.
Mechanic directions (Claude's suggestions; library concept ids in brackets; Kaptain's ideas #8, 9, 22, 24, 30, 31 in Part 4):
- Brutes fed by blood: big units that cost less or grow when your units were damaged or died. [cost_reduction, on_damaged, permanent_growth, count_payoff]
- Blood as fuel: self-damage that also hurts the enemy; some small ways to gain defence from it. [life_payment, friendly_fire, sacrifice, on_damaged]
- Hit hard and burn: direct and area damage, units that strike on Place. [direct_damage, aoe_damage, on_play]
- Protect the wounded front: hit-and-push, so the enemy must spend supply to move and hit. [forced_movement, on_attack]
- Feast on the fallen: gains from enemy deaths. [on_kill, death_trigger]

E3. EXANIMATI — the march of the unburied
------------------------------------------------------------------
STATUS: VERY MUCH up for BIG redesign. The current cards (summary.txt section 11) are a starting point only; any of them can be changed or dropped. Do not limit the faction to what its current cards do.
Identity (AGREED, 10 Oct 2026): death and disease; own non-Greek theme. Imaginative frame (Claude's suggestion): a necropolis on the march. For them death is a door, not an end: the fallen leave something behind, the grave is a second hand, the named dead come back, and plague is a patient tide that rots the living enemy round after round. Boundary (AGREED): remains/corpses belong to the Rotborn, not the Exanimati.
Mechanic directions (Claude's suggestions; library concept ids in brackets):
- Death as a resource: deaths fuel effects, counts of the dead pay off. [death_trigger, sacrifice, fodder, death_count]
- The grave as a second hand: return, resurrect, recycle from the discard pile (a candidate for the resurrection signature, pending the allocation table). [graveyard, revive, recycle]
- Plague: disease that wears enemies down every round and spreads when they die; lasting losses. [damage_over_time, debuff, permanent_loss]
- Haunting: the dead leave spirits (Shades) behind that still act. [summon, death_trigger]
- Drain: taking from the living to sustain the dead. [lifesteal, debuff]

C1. BLITZ — the relentless rotation (aggro + positional)
------------------------------------------------------------------
Idea: wins before the enemy is set up, attacking every round with cheap units that cycle through the front row: strike, step back, the next one steps up.
How it wins: kills front-row holders in rounds 1-3, takes two bases early, and keeps them by refilling the front row faster than the enemy can clear it.
Mechanics:
- Quick (1): attacking costs 1 supply instead of my supply. It can deploy and attack in the same round. [haste, tempo]
- Fall Back (1): after I attack, I may swap places, for free, with the ally directly behind me. The fresh unit takes the dot, the attacker rests in row 2. Rows 2-3 are its queue. [swap, movement, rows]
- Momentum (1): +1 strength this round for each attack your side already made this round. Cheap Quick attacks go first, the Momentum finisher last. [count_payoff, combo_sequence, temporary_buff]
- Rampage (2): once per round, when my strike kills, I can attack again for free. [on_kill, multi_attack]
- Spoils (text): when my strike kills a front-row unit, +1 supply in my lane this round. Kills pay for the next attack. [on_kill, resource_gain]
Self-sufficiency: holding (rotation refills the dots), removal (attacks), supply (Quick, Spoils), cards (spells that draw after many attacks).
Weak to: walls, Last Stand, Riposte.
Examples:
- Raider Scout    1 - 1|1  — Quick.
- Line Runner     1 - 1|1  — Fall Back.
- Looter          2 - 2|2  — Spoils.
- Spear Runner    2 - 2|2  — Momentum.
- Reaver Captain  3 - 3|2  — Rampage.
- War Cry (1) — Pick a lane: your units there gain +2 strength this round.
- Press the Attack (2) — Draw 2 if your side attacked 3 or more times this round.
Spells ~25%.

------------------------------------------------------------------
C2. ARCANISTS — spells are the army (burn + card advantage)
------------------------------------------------------------------
Idea: the units are spell engines; spells deal the damage, draw the cards and get cheaper the more casters stand in a lane.
How it wins: clears front rows with damage spells while sturdy holders grow with every spell cast in their lane.
Mechanics:
- Spell Power N (N): your spells deal +N damage to targets in my lane. Works from any row, so casters stand in rows 2-3. [spell_power, rows]
- Attunement (text): whenever you cast a spell in my lane, I gain +0|+1 (or +1|+0) this round. The holders get sturdier as you cast. [on_spell, temporary_buff]
- Focus (2): your spells cast in my lane cost 1 less (never below 0). [cost_reduction, on_spell]
- Chain: a spell does more if your previous action this round was also a spell. Actions alternate, so chaining gives up board actions. [combo_sequence, on_spell]
- Cantrips: cheap spells that also draw a card, to keep the hand full of spells. [draw, card_flow]
Self-sufficiency: holding (Attunement), removal (spells), supply (Focus), cards (cantrips). [SIGNATURE: many cheap draw spells only if card flow is the Arcanists' signature (allocation table)]
Weak to: hand theft, Toll.
Examples:
- Apprentice    1 - 1|1  — Spell Power 1.
- Warded Adept  2 - 1|3  — Whenever you cast a spell in my lane, I gain +0|+1 this round.
- Focus Lector  3 - 2|3  — Focus.
- Bolt (1) — Deals 2 damage to a unit.
- Arc (2) — Pick a lane: deals 1 damage to every enemy there. Chain: 2 instead.
- Insight (1) — Draw a card. Chain: draw 2.
Spells ~50%.

------------------------------------------------------------------
C3. WARDENS — lock and answer (control)
------------------------------------------------------------------
Idea: stops threats instead of racing them.
How it wins: lets the enemy commit, then binds, silences, bounces or exiles the key units; wins late in two lanes where the enemy can't act.
Mechanics:
- Bind: the unit can't move or attack this round. Buys a round in a contested lane. [disable, cant_attack]
- Silence: the unit loses its card text and keywords, lasting (its stats stay). Shuts engines: Revenant, Plague, round-end abilities. [silence]
- Exile: the unit leaves the game; no Death effects, no return, its card goes nowhere. [banish, removal]
- Bounce: back to its owner's hand as the plain deck card; the owner pays again. [return_to_hand, tempo]
- Toll (2): enemy actions in my lane cost 1 more supply. Slows a whole lane, from any row. [cost_increase, lane_rule]
Self-sufficiency: holding (sturdy low-strength wardens), removal (exile, bounce), supply (Toll taxes the enemy), cards (Interrogate: when I bind a unit, draw a card). [DRAW RULE: limited only if the unit binds once (on Place); check]
Weak to: many cheap units, copies.
Examples:
- Gaoler       2 - 1|2  — Place: binds the enemy facing me.
- Hushkeeper   3 - 2|3  — Place: silences an enemy in my lane.
- Toll Warden  3 - 1|4  — Toll.
- Shackles (1) — Bind an enemy unit.
- Recall (2) — Return a unit with supply 3 or less to its owner's hand.
- Banish (5) — Exile an enemy unit.
Spells ~45%.

------------------------------------------------------------------
C4. MENDERS — refuse to die (sustain + fortress)
------------------------------------------------------------------
Idea: units refuse to die and grow tougher from healing, and every restore also hurts the enemy.
How it wins: its holders keep returning to full defence, so it holds two bases through whole rounds; healing triggers deal the damage.
Mechanics:
- Back-line healers: healers restore the ally directly in front of them (round end, or Place). Healers stand in rows 2-3, holders in front. [heal, rows, turn_end]
- Overheal: defence restored beyond what was lost becomes extra defence above the card, up to +3 (as Drain does). [heal, shield]
- Lifebond (2): when my strike deals damage, the most damaged ally in my lane restores that much defence. [lifesteal]
- Mending triggers (text): whenever an ally in my lane restores defence, I deal 1 damage to the nearest enemy in my column. Healing is removal. [synergy_payoff, ping]
- Last Stand (2): the first time each round I would die, I survive with 1 defence. [cheat_death]
Self-sufficiency: holding, removal (triggers, Lifebond strikes), supply (Pilgrims: Supply 1 healers), cards (Blessing: draw when one action restores 3 or more).
Weak to: lasting defence loss (can't be restored), Exile, Silence.
Examples:
- Field Medic        1 - 0|2  — Round end: the ally directly in front of me restores 1 defence.
- Sister of Mending  2 - 1|2  — Whenever an ally in my lane restores defence, I deal 1 damage to the nearest enemy in my column.
- Bonded Blade       3 - 3|2  — Lifebond.
- Unyielding         3 - 2|3  — Last Stand.
- Wellspring (2) — Pick a lane: your units there restore 2 defence (Overheal applies).
Spells ~30%.

------------------------------------------------------------------
C5. LEGION — strength in numbers (swarm + formation)
------------------------------------------------------------------
Idea: many cheap bodies that are strong together, measured by full rows and full columns of the 3x3 camp.
How it wins: holds dots by numbers in all three lanes; full rows make each body sturdy, mass buffs turn the swarm into an attack.
Mechanics:
- Recruit N (N+1): Place: N Recruit tokens (1|1, supply 0) in free cells of my camp. [summon, fodder, board_presence]
- Full Row (text): a bonus while all 3 cells of my row are filled ("+1|+1 while my row is full"). [row_effects, condition_count]
- Deep Column (text): a bonus while all 3 cells of my column are filled ("the front unit of my column has +2 defence"). Rows 2-3 hold the units that make the front one strong. [link_formation, condition_count]
- Muster (2): +1 strength for each other ally in my camp. [count_payoff, board_presence]
- Rally: spells, every unit you control in a lane gains +1|+1 this round. [buff, temporary_buff, scope_multi]
Self-sufficiency: holding (numbers, full rows), removal (Muster, Rally), supply (cheap bodies; Quartermaster: Supply +1 while my camp is full), cards (Roll Call: draw if one of your camps is full).
Weak to: lane-wide damage, effects that kill the weakest unit.
Examples:
- Shield Brother      1 - 1|1  — +1|+1 while my row is full.
- Conscript Sergeant  2 - 1|1  — Recruit 1.
- Column Captain      3 - 2|3  — While my column is full, its front unit has +2 defence.
- Drillmaster         3 - 2|3  — Muster.
- Call to Arms (2) — Pick a lane: places 2 Recruits in free cells of your camp there.
- Rally (1) — Pick a lane: your units there gain +1|+1 this round.
Spells ~35%.

C6. TITANS — few, huge, crushing (big units + ramp)
------------------------------------------------------------------
Idea: plays few units that cover several cells; one titan in the front row holds a whole base alone (a ▪▪▪ covers all three dots).
How it wins: ramps into titans in two lanes; each covers enough dots to own its base and crushes what faces it.
Mechanics:
- Lumbering (drawback, -2): moving me costs twice my supply. Titans are placed where they will stay; the drawback buys their size. [drawback, movement]
- Stomp N (N): Place: I deal N damage to each enemy facing my cells. Clears the facing front row on arrival. [aoe_damage, on_play]
- Shelter (text): allies directly behind me can't be struck. Small casters and supply units live behind the titan. [protection, rows]
- Forerunners (text): small units, "the next unit of size 3 or more you place this round costs 2 less". [cost_reduction]
- Quake (text): round end: I deal 1 damage to each enemy in the facing front row. [turn_end, ping]
Self-sufficiency: holding (bodies), removal (Stomp, Quake), supply (Forerunners; Herdkeeper: Supply +1), cards (Call the Titan: draw a unit of size 3 or more from your deck).
Weak to: Bounce, Exile, Bind, lasting defence loss.
Examples:
- Herald of Giants  1 - 1|1        — Place: the next unit of size 3 or more you place this round costs 1 less.
- Ancient           4 - 3|4  ▪▪    — Lumbering. Shelter.
- Stone Colossus    5 - 5|5  ▪▪▪   — Lumbering.
- Earthshaker       6 - 5|4  ▪▪▪▪  — Lumbering. Stomp 2.
- Titan's Stride (2) — Pick a lane: your units of size 2 or more there gain +2|+2 this round.
Spells ~20%.

C7. TREASURY — save, then buy big (ramp + kicker)
------------------------------------------------------------------
Idea: weak early, banks supply, then plays expensive cards or the paid-up version of cheap ones.
How it wins: survives the first rounds with cheap defenders that grow while it saves, then spends the bank on 7-10 supply units or Surplus effects that swing two lanes.
Mechanics:
- Vault (2): your carry-over cap is 2 higher while I'm in your back row. [resource_banking, round_carryover]
- Hoard (text; moved from the dropped Tempo idea): round end: I gain +1|+1 (lasting) for every 2 supply left unspent in my lane. Saving makes its holders grow. [resource_banking, permanent_growth]
- Surplus N: when you play the card, you may pay N more; then its Surplus effect happens too. Each card is a cheap play now or a strong one later. [kicker]
- Interest (text): round end: +1 carry-over if you have 3 or more. [round_carryover, resource_gain]
- Big buys: units of 7-10 supply with plain strong stats. [finisher]
Self-sufficiency: holding (Hoard units), removal (Surplus effects, big buys), supply (its core), cards (Buy Intelligence: draw 2; Surplus draws).
Weak to: early aggression, theft.
Examples:
- Vault Keeper     2 - 0|3  — Vault.
- Miser            2 - 1|3  — Hoard.
- Hired Blade      2 - 2|3  — Surplus 2: Place: I deal 2 damage to the enemy facing me.
- Moneylender      3 - 1|4  — Interest.
- Gilded Colossus  8 - 8|8.
- Windfall (1) — +2 carry-over.
Spells ~30%.

------------------------------------------------------------------
C8. BASTION — the unbreakable line (fortress + punishment)
------------------------------------------------------------------
Idea: you can't break through it, and trying hurts.
How it wins: holds dots with walls and screened units, kills attackers with defence-based strikes; slow, wins by holding two bases through rounds.
Mechanics:
- Palisades: tokens 0|3, Immobile, supply 0, made in front-row cells; they hold dots. [summon, blocking]
- Screen (2): I can't be struck while an ally stands directly in front of me. Strikers and supply sit safe in rows 2-3 behind the walls. [protection, rows]
- Hold (2): enemies placed or moved into the cells facing me can't attack this round. [on_enemy_enter, cant_attack]
- Brace (1): my strikes use my defence instead of my strength. Its high-defence units are its attackers. [stat_swap]
- Unbroken (text): round end: if I took no damage this round, I gain +1 defence (lasting). [permanent_growth, turn_end]
Self-sufficiency: holding, removal (Brace), supply (Garrison: Supply +1 for each Palisade in my lane, up to 2), cards (Fortify: draw a card and make a Palisade).
Weak to: lasting defence loss, units displaced to other lanes, control.
Examples:
- Wallwright     2 - 1|2  — Place: a Palisade in a free cell of your front row.
- Gate Sentinel  3 - 2|3  — Hold.
- Tower Shield   3 - 1|5  — Brace.
- Raise the Walls (2) — Pick a lane: a Palisade in each free cell of your front row there.
Spells ~20%.

C9. ASCENDANTS — grow in safety, then step forward (scaling)
------------------------------------------------------------------
Idea: units grow while protected in the back rows and step into the front row when they are big.
How it wins: a few units grow each round end in rows 2-3; once large they move to the front and hold dots nobody can break.
Mechanics:
- Nurture (2): round end: I gain +1|+1 (lasting) if I'm not in your front row. The back rows become the nursery. [permanent_growth, rows]
- Mentors (text): round end, the ally directly in front of me gains +1|+1 (lasting). [buff, permanent_growth]
- Veteran triggers (text): +1|+1 when I kill or survive damage. [on_kill, on_damaged, permanent_growth]
- Ascend (text): when my strength reaches N, my second line of text switches on (a keyword, an aura). A condition, not a counter. [stat_condition, upgrade]
- Inheritance (text): Death: an adjacent ally gains my lasting gains. Removal loses its sting. [death_trigger, donate]
Self-sufficiency: holding (grown units), removal (grown attackers), supply (Granary: Supply +1 while in your back row), cards (Lesson: draw a card when one of your units ascends).
Weak to: Silence, Exile, early aggression.
Examples:
- Seedling Squire  1 - 0|1  — Nurture.
- Tutor            2 - 1|2  — Round end: the ally directly in front of me gains +1|+1.
- Disciple         2 - 1|2  — Ascend at 4 strength: I gain Pike and +2 defence.
- Heir             3 - 2|3  — Inheritance.
- Growth Spurt (2) — Double a unit's lasting stat gains.
Spells ~25%.

------------------------------------------------------------------
C10. SUNDERERS — break defence for good (defence-lowering + punishment)
------------------------------------------------------------------
Idea: enemy defence falls and stays fallen; its holders crack whoever strikes them.
How it wins: enemy holders lose defence that restoring can't bring back until anything kills them; ordinary strikes then take the dots.
Mechanics:
- Sunder N: the target loses N defence, lasting (like Creeping Rot's -2 strength, but defence). Restoring can't undo it; at 0 the unit dies. [permanent_loss, debuff]
- Pierce (2): Armoured doesn't block my strikes, and Bodyguard doesn't take them. [piercing]
- Cracking Hide (text): when I'm struck, the attacker is Sundered 1. Its defenders punish attackers. [counterattack, on_damaged]
- Exposed payoffs (text): +2 damage to units whose defence is below their printed defence. [stat_condition, attack_mod]
- Shatter (text): when a Sundered enemy dies, the enemies adjacent to it are Sundered 1. [death_trigger, debuff]
Self-sufficiency: holding (Cracking Hide), removal, supply (Salvage: when a Sundered enemy dies, +1 supply in its lane), cards (spells that draw on a kill).
Weak to: many small units, growth.
Examples:
- Chisel     1 - 1|1  — My strikes Sunder 1.
- Thornhide  2 - 1|3  — Cracking Hide.
- Ram Crew   3 - 3|2  — Pierce.
- Breaker    3 - 3|2  — My strikes deal +2 damage to units below their printed defence.
- Splinter (2) — Sunder 2 an enemy unit.
- Fracture (3) — Pick an enemy unit: Sunder 1 it and every enemy in its row.
Spells ~40%.

------------------------------------------------------------------
C11. MEASURERS — exact numbers (precision + control)
------------------------------------------------------------------
Idea: picks exact amounts and rewrites stats; rewarded for exactness and balance.
How it wins: neutralises the enemy's biggest units by setting or swapping their stats, kills with exact amounts, holds with balanced units.
Mechanics:
- Choose X: X spells and units, X picked up to your available supply. [kicker, variable_stats]
- Set: set a unit's strength or defence to N (this round cheaply, lasting for more). [variable_stats, debuff]
- Swap: swap a unit's strength and defence. Your 6|1 attacker becomes a 1|6 holder; the enemy's 1|6 wall becomes a 6|1 target. [stat_swap]
- Exact (2): if my strike kills with nothing left over, draw a card. [excess_damage, draw]
- Balance (text): a bonus while my strength equals my defence. [stat_condition]
Self-sufficiency: holding (Swap, Balance), removal (Set, exact strikes), supply (Exact Change: an action that leaves exactly 0 supply in its lane gives +1 carry-over), cards (Exact).
Weak to: many targets, copies.
Examples:
- Gauger        2 - 2|1  — Exact.
- Scale-Keeper  3 - 2|2  — While my strength equals my defence, I have +1|+1 and Armoured.
- Measured Cut (X) — Deals X damage to a unit.
- Invert (1) — Swap a unit's strength and defence this round.
- Equalise (2) — Set an enemy unit's strength to 1 this round.
Spells ~50%.

------------------------------------------------------------------
C12. MONOLITHS — never moves, owns its columns (every unit Immobile; Kaptain's idea)
------------------------------------------------------------------
Idea: every unit is Immobile and sturdier for it; its units reach down whole columns and stop the enemy moving.
How it wins: places each monolith once, in the right cell; they strike the whole facing enemy column and lock the lane, so the enemy can't fix its line.
Mechanics:
- Immobile on every unit (its -2 gives each unit +2 stats). [drawback, movement]
- Column Reach (2): my strikes reach every cell of my column in the enemy camp. A back-row monolith attacks the enemy's back rows. [ranged, lanes]
- Lock (text): enemies in my lane can't move. [disable, zone_of_control]
- Foundations (text): +1|+1 for each adjacent Immobile ally. Monoliths are built in blocks. [adjacency, link_formation]
- Placement as movement: Place effects that let the next unit you play this round be placed in any lane or any cell, the faction's only way to choose position late. [placement_rules, cross_lane]
Self-sufficiency: holding (sturdy), removal (Column Reach), supply (Quarry: Supply +1), cards (Inscription: Place: draw a card if I'm adjacent to two allies).
Weak to: units displaced to other lanes, pushes.
Examples:
- Standing Stone  1 - 1|4.
- Keystone        3 - 3|4  — Foundations.
- Obelisk         3 - 2|5  — Column Reach.
- Warden Stone    4 - 2|6  — Lock.
- Seal the Lane (1) — Pick a lane: enemies there can't move this round.
Spells ~30%.

------------------------------------------------------------------
C13. FORGE — builds constructs, arms its units (assembly + items; Kaptain's items idea)
------------------------------------------------------------------
Idea: builds big constructs out of parts and arms its units with items; parts and items keep their value when units die.
How it wins: assembles constructs in its front rows (a construct covers 2-3 cells and holds a base), arms its holders, re-arms them when they fall.
Mechanics:
- Parts and Assembly (NEW RULE, like the Stitching Table): Part units name a recipe ("a Frame, an Engine and a Weapon in one row"); when the recipe stands complete, the parts merge in place into the Construct printed on them (their combined stats plus its ability). A board condition, not a counter. [merge_attach, card_crafting]

5. CARD DESIGN METHOD (11 Oct 2026; Claude's proposal after Kaptain's critique of the three current factions)
==================================================================
Base-bound cards (R1): the condition is the base of the unit's own lane (AGREED, 11 Oct 2026).
Base bonuses: each game the bases are assigned at random from a large pool, so cards must never depend on one specific base bonus; they check only "you hold / don't hold this lane's base" (AGREED, 11 Oct 2026).
Steps for every faction:
1. Read cards, not statistics: the 30-50 most characteristic cards of each source faction (small-set games and Marvel Snap included); write each card's play pattern (the decision it creates, what it rewards) and its Three Bases translation.
2. Engine map: one idea; enablers -> payoffs drawn as a web. Every payoff has 3+ enablers in the faction, every enabler feeds 2+ payoffs; an enabler that also hurts the enemy beats one that only hurts you.
3. A moment per card: when, where and why to play it must be a real decision; at most ~15% plain keyword-plus-stats bodies.
4. Interaction share: cards that touch the enemy or answer what the enemy does (on enemy placed, on struck, survives damage, punish). The share differs per faction: playful/disruptive factions high, straightforward factions lower (AGREED, 11 Oct 2026).
5. Card variety and signatures (AGREED, 11 Oct 2026):
   - Each faction: 3-5 core mechanics (mechanics, not keywords; keywords only enable mechanics), and its own keyword set. Not every faction gets every mechanic or keyword.
   - Signature mechanics (card copying, deck search/tutor, draw bonanza, card creation, forced movement, supply play, resurrection, bonds, stealing cards, etc.) belong to 1, at most 2, factions, where they are prominent. Elsewhere they appear only on a very few cards, and only when expressed through that faction's own character (example: a HIT HIT faction gets ONE card that resurrects its STRONGEST dead unit).
   - Draw: every faction has at least one draw card, tied to its most basic signature (one draw card is enough for most factions).
   - Draw is RARE and BOUNDED (AGREED, 11 Oct 2026): draw sits on an effect that fires at most once per card: a spell, a Place effect, the unit's OWN death (watch resurrection/return factions, where a death can repeat), or an explicit "once per round" cap. NEVER on a trigger tied to repeated or shared events ("whenever a friendly unit survives damage / a spell is cast, draw"). Same for other card advantage (generate, copy, tutor) outside its signature faction.
   - Also: Place effects, triggers, round-end, base-bound cards, spells in several roles, a few deliberate gap-fillers that are strong without an obvious drawback.
6. The game's own levers: alternating single actions (sequencing), lane-locked supply, rows (a back-row job for every faction), two of three bases held through a whole round.
   - Lane control conditions (AGREED, 11 Oct 2026): "if you hold this lane's base", "if you don't", and cards with an effect for each case. Condition checks the current state ("as long as you hold"), not "held since round start".
7. Checks: costing check (summary.txt section 2), network line (which inputs it uses or needs), FREQUENCY check: classify each triggered effect by what bounds it: once per card (Place, own death, spell), once per round (explicit cap, round-end of one unit), or unbounded (fires on other units' or repeated events: any friendly hit, any spell cast, any death). Card-advantage and resource effects must be bounded unless the faction's signature is built on them. Real counts come from the simulator (draws, triggers per game).
8. Measure: the headless simulator (engine + Benchmark, ~1 s per game at depth 1): premade decks against each other; per card: games played, win rate when played, damage, kills, how often its ability fired; fix or cut cards that never matter. Caveat: the Benchmark plays synergies only by accident.
