==================================================================
10. WHAT OUTSIDE DESIGN GUIDES TEACH (3 Nov 2026; Claude's reading, after Kaptain's critique of the five new factions)
==================================================================
Each guide: what it says (in short), whether it helps Three Bases, and what it means here. The rules taken from it are in
factions/START_DESIGN.md section 2b. Ideas marked "Claude's" are not from the guide.

A. STRENGTHS, LIMITS, WEAKNESSES (Hearthstone, "Developer Insights: Class Identity", 2019)
- Each class has a fantasy, things it is good at, things it may do only a few times at low power, and things it cannot do.
  Weaknesses matter as much as strengths: they let the designers push the strengths further, and they let the enemy plan
  before seeing a card. Two cards were removed from the game only because they gave a class something on its weak list.
- Helps: yes. Our faction entries list what a faction does, never what it must not do. Three Bases: every faction gets a
  "weak at" line and a "only a little" line. Example of the miss: melee buffs (Dean of Battle Magic) on the Arcanists.

B. TRAITS BECOME MECHANICS; CONTRAST; TOOLS AGAINST EACH OTHER (Rosewater, "Battle Plans", 2015)
- Pick about five traits of the faction (the Eldrazi: ancient, huge, alien, linked, hungry) and turn each into a mechanic.
  Then make the factions contrast (big and slow against small and fast). Give each faction a plan with a weak spot the
  others can hit, and a few cards that punish the others. Let cards of two factions work together quietly, or decks repeat.
- Helps: yes. Our docs have identity first, but no step that turns traits into mechanics, no contrast between factions,
  and no tools against the others.

C. WHAT A FACTION NEEDS (Rosewater, "Faction Packed", 2018)
- A faction with no mechanical identity is not seen as a faction at all. Speed, curve, win route, strengths and weaknesses
  must all match who they are. Decide what the player should feel. Factions that share decks need to work together.
  Across all factions cover fast and slow, threats and answers, unit-heavy and spell-heavy.
- Helps: partly (most is already in our method). New: "what should the player feel" in each entry.

D. SAME HOOK, DIFFERENT USE (Rosewater, Strixhaven Part 2, 2021)
- Five factions shared one trigger (casting a spell), and each used it its own way: one grew its units, one built to one
  big spell, one gained and spent life. Each faction was built on a tension inside itself, not on what its parts share.
- Helps: yes, for mixed decks later. Three Bases: a few shared hooks (a unit dies, a spell is cast, a unit enters a lane)
  that each faction answers in its own way. This is how two-faction decks can work without copying cards.

E. FINDING A MECHANIC AND ITS LIMITS (Rosewater, "Nuts & Bolts #17", 2025)
- Mechanics come from a need the set has, a quality cards already share, or a problem found in testing. Before designing
  cards, write the mechanic's space: which cards can have it, when it happens, what that timing rules out ("an end-of-turn
  trigger does not go with a temporary combat boost"). Solve problems with mechanics, not with more power. Build test
  decks early.
- Helps: yes. The timing question is exactly the "-1 strength this round" mistake: in Three Bases the enemy can simply not
  attack that round, and units almost never move, so "can't move this round" is close to nothing.

F. A MECHANIC THAT NEEDS PARTNERS NEEDS MANY CARDS (Rosewater, "Nuts & Bolts #12", 2020)
- A mechanic that works alone plays well anywhere; one that needs partner cards needs many of them. Count how many cards
  of each mechanic a deck holds. Write a placeholder for each need ("a card that brings this unit into the enemy camp")
  before the cards exist, and fill it.
- Helps: yes. The Sapper in a deck that cannot carry him to the enemy camp is a missing placeholder. Three Bases: with 2
  draws a round and 30-40 cards, a cash-in card needs enough setup cards in the deck to be in hand in time.

G. MECHANICS THAT MULTIPLY (Goblin Artisans, "Multiplicative design", 2018)
- One element that works with many others beats one that works with one (a key opens one door; wind moves enemies, fire,
  sails). Wide triggers ("when a unit dies here") link to more cards than narrow ones ("when a Projectile dies").
  Mechanics with many contact points link best. A mechanic that needs partners still becomes a web if it has enough cards
  and the cards interact in different ways; tribes with no crossing cards stayed lines (Ixalan).
- Helps: yes, the most. This is Kaptain's point that cards must belong to two families and be parts of chains.

H. CARDS THAT ARE BOTH SETUP AND CASH-IN (Riptide Lab forum, "Bosses vs leaders")
- Some cards wait for others to do the work and give nothing themselves; others are part of the theme and gain from it
  (an enchantment that rewards enchantments also sets off the other enchantment cards). Also: how good a card is alone
  against with its partners. A deck wants many cards of the second kind and a few of the first.
- Helps: yes. Three Bases: prefer cards that set up one mechanic and cash in another (a wall that is also a Projectile).

I. EVERY CARD HAS A PURPOSE; CUT HARD (Slay the Spire, Giovannetti, GDC 2019)
- Cards need not be equally strong, but each must be a build-around or a staple. They made thousands of ideas and cut most
  of them; arguments were settled by play data (win rate, pick rate per card).
- Helps: partly; method step 8 (simulator) already says this. New: cut twins. Two cards with the same effect at the same
  cost (Spell Conduit and Star Seer) means one goes.

J. ATTITUDE (Rosewater, "Twenty Years, Twenty Lessons", GDC 2016)
- Restrictions breed creativity; interesting is not the same as fun; a game everyone likes and no one loves fails; a small
  change can change everything. Helps: only as attitude.

K. THE GAME'S OWN RULES DECIDE WHAT IS STRONG (Runeterra)
- Their designers said protection and buff spells are much better than removal in their game, because champions level up
  over several turns. Players also said regions built for pairs lost their own identity.
- Helps: yes, as a warning. Never take a card's value from another game; check it on this board (START_DESIGN 1b). Keep
  each faction's core whole on its own; mixing hooks stay small.

What did not help: general "how to design a card game" pages and AI prompt pages (no method), mana curve and rarity advice
(Three Bases has lane supply and no rarity), draft signposts (no draft here). Claude's idea from signposts: the 5 special
units of a faction could each point to one way to play it.
