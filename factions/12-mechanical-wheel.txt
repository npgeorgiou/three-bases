==================================================================
12. THE MECHANICAL WHEEL: designing 20 factions together
==================================================================
Why: designing factions one by one from a theme loses what makes each faction unique and how its mechanics connect.
Each faction's group of concepts, identity and Kaptain's seven questions come first for all 20 factions at once; the
theme comes after the group, as a source of each faction's tools. The wheel is made by calculation, research and intuition. The steps, in order, with their
stops: factions/START_DESIGN-1-wheel.md. Commands: factions/wheel/PIPELINE.md.

Principles
1. The wheel is a start for exploring: factions are painted on a circle of radii, and the radii that work together
   are found. Each faction's group of library concepts comes after, from its radii; its mechanics are the links real
   cards show between the group's concepts. Factions have no neighbours; the circle only shows the radii.
2. A faction is 3-6 strong radii joined by trigger -> payoff (one radius makes what another needs), wherever they sit
   on the circle. Real factions do the same: of the 27 with a value on 20+ radii, 19 are strong on 3-6 radii, only 3
   have them side by side, and opposite radii are not kept apart (correlation 0.01). No two factions sit on the same
   place.
3. A faction builds on a group of concepts (2 pillars, 2-4 others), turned into 2-4 tools that feed each other; its
   cards use its list (every usable concept and helper) a lot, to serve the group.
4. Every faction differs from every other in each of the seven answers.
5. Combos are trigger -> payoff between factions: a card of one makes the thing a card of another gains from. Every
   faction combines well with at least 10 others. Planned combos are only part of the hooks; each faction's chat adds
   more. Shared cards (one card in two factions) are one source of combos, never the only one.
6. A graph check: concepts are nodes, a line where real cards of one make what cards of another use; each faction's
   group is connected.
7. Each faction, once built, is measured on the radii (`node tools/wheel_check.js <letter> "<planned values>"`): it
   must land where the wheel put it; if it drifted, the faction is fixed, not the wheel.

The axes. On the page the ends below are split into 23 radii in 8 areas (factions/13-wheel-measurement.txt), measured
by axes.py on 43 factions of 8 games (Marvel Snap has no factions; Monster Train is a different kind of game).
1.  Own units: spends them (deaths, sacrifice, trading a unit for value) or keeps them alive (heal, more defence,
    shields, protection, grow).
2.  Disruption: breaks the enemy's plan (their hand, costs, abilities, attacks, positions, spells) or only kills units.
3.  Where the damage comes from: units' own strikes and growth, or spells and fixed damage.
4.  Supply and cards: income, carry-over, draws, cheaper cards; or units on the board now.
    Axes 4, 5 and 8 are measured at their first end only: a low value there is the second end.
5.  Works with other cards (a positional combo is a kind of combo): units that gain from the allies beside or behind
    them, and cards that make what other cards of the faction need; or cards that work alone.
6.  How many targets: many at once and counting the board, or one precise target.
7.  Width: many cheap units and cards that make units, or a few big ones.
8.  Hand and discard pile: picks, copies and brings back its own cards, or plays what it draws.
9.  Unit stats: strength and defence per supply, two radii; a faction's lean is the strength value minus the defence
    value. Games with one number for both (Stormbound) count it as both.
A game with under 10 cards for a radius gives its factions no value on it, not 0.

Not radii, and why (don't reintroduce):
- Sets the pace or answers: most library games have no answering on the enemy's turn; it only repeats removal.
- Early or late: can't be measured (right in only 63 of 149 Faeria deck pairs).
- Moving: most library games have no moving. Repair or prevent: only places factions that keep units.
- Rows and areas: "one cell or a row" repeats axis 6; "front or back rows" is a design question per faction.
- What cards react to (placed, attacking, hit, dying): a list, not a scale, covered by axes 1 and 3.
- How hard it is to play; locks enemies or outmoves them.
- Timing (players act now; round end is built FOR, not waited for), staying in one lane (every faction must win 2 of 3
  lanes), random effects (not allowed), this-round effects (worth little).

Reading the numbers:
- Which ends go together is judged by play: one end makes what the other needs, or undoes it (library fed/feeds/both).
  A correlation across real factions only shows what those games did, never that two ends can't work (damaging or
  killing your own units to set off effects, locking enemies while moving: all sound plays).
- Identity also lives in the cost curve, the stats and how a faction feels to play, not only in what cards do.
- How it is measured and how to redo it for new games or changed library cards: factions/13-wheel-measurement.txt.
