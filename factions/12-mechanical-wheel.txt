==================================================================
12. THE MECHANICAL WHEEL: designing 20 factions together (AGREED with Kaptain, 5 Nov 2026)
==================================================================
Why: designing factions one by one from a theme lost what makes each faction unique and how its mechanics connect.
Mechanics, identity and Kaptain's five questions come first for all 20 factions at once; the theme comes last, per
faction, as inspiration. The wheel is made by calculation, research and intuition; it starts empty (the seven factions
in the game are not placed on it; their themes may be reused later).

Method (Claude's proposal, agreed in outline):
1. A pool of mechanics from the library, before any faction: each written "I do X -> I gain from X" on this game's
   basic things, each able to carry 30+ cards on this board.
2. The 20 factions as nodes on a circle. Each mechanic has one owner (its monopoly); up to its two neighbours may use it
   on a few cards; the far side of the wheel never has it (its blind spots).
3. The factions are spread along the axes below, so neighbours differ and opposites differ most.
4. A table: the 20 factions by their answers to the five questions; every faction differs from every other in each.
5. A graph check: mechanics are nodes, a line where one makes what another needs (library fed/feeds); each faction's
   mechanics form one connected group, with lines to each neighbour.
6. The wheel lives in one table file with a check script; the picture is drawn from the table.
7. Then each faction is designed in its own chat (factions/START_DESIGN.md), its theme chosen to fit.

The axes (first measured on 39 real factions of the 8 library games in an earlier chat, with no saved script; now measured
by axes.py below. Changed 9 Nov 2026 by Kaptain: "locks enemies or outmoves them", "what its cards react to" (placed,
attacking, hit, dying: a list of four kinds, not a scale, and covered by axes 1, 3 and 14) and "how hard it is to play"
dropped; disruption added):
1.  Own units: spends them (deaths, sacrifice, trading a unit for value) or keeps them (protect, heal, grow).
2.  Disruption (Kaptain, 9 Nov 2026): breaks the enemy's plan (their hand, costs, abilities, attacks, positions, spells)
    or only kills their units.
3.  Where the damage comes from: units' own strikes and growth, or spells and fixed damage.
4.  Supply and cards: income, carry-over, draws, cheaper cards; or units on the board now.
5.  Formation: units that gain from the allies beside or behind them, or units that work alone.
6.  How many targets: many at once and counting the board, or one precise target.
7.  Unit size: few big costly units, or many cheap ones.
8.  Moving: moves its own units and pushes enemies around, or stays put.
9.  Repair or prevent: restores lost shields and defence after a hit, or stops the hit beforehand.
10. Hand and discard pile: picks, copies and brings back its own cards, or plays what it draws.
11. Rows and areas: works from the front row or the back rows; hits one cell or a whole row or column (the row part is
    this board's own, not measured).
12. Speed (Kaptain, 5 Nov 2026): how quickly the faction carries out its plan; seen in the cost curve (Faeria Yellow
    averages cost 3.1 against Red 4.8).
13. Strength or defence: its units lean to killing or to holding dots (defence holds a base); seen in the stats.
14. Sets the pace or answers: forces the enemy to react to its plan, or waits and punishes what the enemy does.
Open (Claude's suggestions, 9 Nov 2026, not decided): axes 7 and 12 both come from cost; axis 9 only places factions that
keep units; axis 11's "one cell or a row" repeats axis 6; combos (cards that need each other) as their own axis or inside
axis 5; factions of games without moving get no value on axis 8; how to measure axis 14.
How the axes were missed: the first measured axes came only from what cards do (concept shares), so identity that lives
in the cost curve, the stats and how a faction feels to play was invisible; look there too.
Which ends go together is judged by play: one end makes what the other needs, or undoes it (library fed/feeds/both).
A correlation across real factions only shows what those games happened to do, never that two ends can't work
(damaging or killing your own units to set off effects, locking enemies while moving, keeping a big unit alive by
repairing it: all sound plays).
Not axes: timing (players act now; round end is built FOR, not waited for), staying in one lane (every faction must
win 2 of 3 lanes), random effects (not allowed), lasting or this-round effects (this-round effects are worth little).

The measurement (9 Nov 2026): `library/cards_db/axes.py` measures every axis on a scale for the 49 real factions of the
library (10+ cards each) and writes `library/cards_db/axes.tsv` and `factions/wheel-axes.html` (circle of axes, map of
factions, numbers, closest factions; template `tools/wheel_axes_template.html`). Which concepts measure each end is
Claude's choice, written in axes.py and shown on hover in the page; change it there and rerun. Score = share of cards with
the high end minus share with the low end (axes 7, 13, 14 from cost and stats), centred within the game, then in steps of
spread over all factions. Two counts: all cards, or cards weighted by how many decks of decks.tsv play them (factions with
fewer than 10 cards in decks get no deck count). The rows part of axis 11 is not measured.
The numbers quoted above came from an earlier chat with no saved script; this script gives Faeria Yellow 3.1 and Red 4.8
average cost as quoted, but Runeterra Shurima 3.0 / Freljord 4.3 (quoted 2.9 / 4.1) and Duelyst Lyonar 31% / Abyssian 43% strength (quoted 36% / 51%).
