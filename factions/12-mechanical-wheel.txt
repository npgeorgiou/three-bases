==================================================================
12. THE MECHANICAL WHEEL: designing 20 factions together
==================================================================
Why: designing factions one by one from a theme loses what makes each faction unique and how its cards connect.
Each faction's archetypes, extras, seven answers, partners, game plan and keywords come first, for all 20 factions at
once; the theme comes right after the archetypes. The steps, in order, with their stops:
factions/START_DESIGN-1-wheel.md. Commands: factions/wheel/PIPELINE.md.

Principles
1. The map: concepts are nodes, and a line joins two concepts that real cards link (factions/wheel-archetypes.html).
   Each archetype is a set of parts on it; a faction plays 2 or 3 archetypes that link strongly, and its synergies are
   the links real cards show between their concepts.
2. A faction is strong at what its archetypes do. Its extras come from its list (every other concept, scored by its
   real links with the archetypes' concepts): first for the seven answers its archetypes don't give, then to feed its
   archetypes' engines.
3. Every faction differs from every other in each of the seven answers.
4. Combos are trigger -> payoff between factions: a card of one makes the thing a card of another gains from. Every
   faction combines well with at least 7 others. Shared keywords are the main source of combos; every faction also
   has at least 1 keyword of its own. Each faction's chat adds more hooks.
5. Each faction, once built, is checked against its archetypes and extras; if it
   drifted, the faction is fixed, not the wheel.
6. The radii below show how the 43 real factions spread; nothing is designed from them.

The axes. On the page the ends below are split into 23 radii in 8 areas (factions/13-wheel-measurement.txt), measured
by axes.py on 43 factions of 8 games (Marvel Snap has no factions; Monster Train is a different kind of game). Every
usable concept belongs to one radius; a faction's value on it is the share of its concept instances there (one card
with two effects puts one instance on each of two radii).
1.  Own units: spends them (deaths, sacrifice, trading a unit for value) or keeps them alive (heal, more defence,
    shields, protection, grow).
2.  Disruption: breaks the enemy's plan (their hand, costs, abilities, attacks, positions, spells) or only kills units.
3.  Where the damage comes from: units' own strikes and growth, or spells and fixed damage.
4.  Supply and cards: income, carry-over, draws, cheaper cards; or units on the board now.
    Axes 4, 5 and 8 are measured at their first end only: a low value there is the second end.
5.  Works with other cards (a positional combo is a kind of combo): units that gain from the allies beside or behind
    them, units that move, and cards that make what other cards of the faction need; or cards that work alone.
6.  How many targets: many at once and counting the board, or one precise target.
7.  Width: many units on my side (cheap units and cards that make units), or a few big ones.
8.  Hand and discard pile: picks its cards and reuses cards, or plays what it draws.
9.  Unit stats: strength and defence per supply, two radii; a faction's lean is the strength value minus the defence
    value. Games with one number for both (Stormbound) count it as both.
A game with under 10 concept instances for a radius gives its factions no value on it, not 0.

Not radii, and why (don't reintroduce):
- Sets the pace or answers: most library games have no answering on the enemy's turn; it only repeats removal.
- Early or late: can't be measured (right in only 63 of 149 Faeria deck pairs).
- Moving as a radius of its own: most library games have no moving; moving my units belongs to "position and combos".
- Repair or prevent: only places factions that keep units.
- Rows and areas: "one cell or a row" repeats axis 6; "front or back rows" is a design question per faction.
- What cards react to (placed, attacking, hit, dying): a list, not a scale, covered by axes 1 and 3.
- How hard it is to play; locks enemies or outmoves them.
- Timing (players act now; round end is built FOR, not waited for), staying in one lane (every faction must win 2 of 3
  lanes), random effects (not allowed), this-round effects (worth little).

Reading the numbers:
- Values compare a faction with the average faction of its own game (1 = average), so compare factions of different games
  loosely. Into the Breach, Stormbound, Slay the Spire and Inscryption have no value on 9 or more radii. Strength, defence
  and the two width radii give a faction its shape; they are not plans.
- Which ends go together is judged by play: one end makes what the other needs, or undoes it (library fed/feeds/both).
  A correlation across real factions only shows what those games did, never that two ends can't work (damaging or
  killing your own units to set off effects, locking enemies while moving: all sound plays).
- Identity also lives in the cost curve, the stats and how a faction feels to play, not only in what cards do.
- How it is measured and how to redo it for new games or changed library cards: factions/13-wheel-measurement.txt.
