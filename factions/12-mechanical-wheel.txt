==================================================================
12. THE MECHANICAL WHEEL: designing 20 factions together (AGREED with Kaptain, 5 Nov 2026)
==================================================================
Why: designing factions one by one from a theme lost what makes each faction unique and how its mechanics connect.
Mechanics, identity and Kaptain's five questions come first for all 20 factions at once; the theme comes last, per
faction, as inspiration. The wheel is made by calculation, research and intuition; it starts empty (the seven factions
in the game are not placed on it; their themes may be reused later).

Method (Claude's proposal, agreed in outline):
1. A pool of mechanics from the library, before any faction: each written "I do X -> I gain from X" on this game's
   basic things, each able to carry 30+ cards on this board.
2. The 20 factions as nodes on a circle. Each mechanic has one owner (its monopoly); up to its two neighbours may use it
   on a few cards; the far side of the wheel never has it (its blind spots).
3. The factions are spread along the axes below, so neighbours differ and opposites differ most.
4. A table: the 20 factions by their answers to the five questions; every faction differs from every other in each.
5. A graph check: mechanics are nodes, a line where one makes what another needs (library fed/feeds); each faction's
   mechanics form one connected group, with lines to each neighbour.
6. The wheel lives in one table file with a check script; the picture is drawn from the table.
7. Then each faction is designed in its own chat (factions/START_DESIGN.md), its theme chosen to fit.

The axes (first measured on 39 real factions of the 8 library games in an earlier chat, with no saved script; now measured
by axes.py below. Changed 9 Nov 2026 by Kaptain: "locks enemies or outmoves them", "what its cards react to" (placed,
attacking, hit, dying: a list of four kinds, not a scale, and covered by axes 1 and 3) and "how hard it is to play"
dropped; disruption added):
1.  Own units: spends them (deaths, sacrifice, trading a unit for value) or keeps them alive: staying power (heal, more
    defence, shields, protection, grow; Kaptain 9 Nov 2026: healing and preservation must be measured).
2.  Disruption (Kaptain, 9 Nov 2026): breaks the enemy's plan (their hand, costs, abilities, attacks, positions, spells)
    or only kills their units.
3.  Where the damage comes from: units' own strikes and growth, or spells and fixed damage.
4.  Supply and cards: income, carry-over, draws, cheaper cards; or units on the board now.
5.  Works with other cards (Kaptain, 9 Nov 2026: a positional combo is a kind of combo, so one axis): units that gain
    from the allies beside or behind them, and cards that make what other cards of the faction need; or cards that work
    alone.
6.  How many targets: many at once and counting the board, or one precise target.
7.  Width (agreed 9 Nov 2026): many cheap units and cards that make units, or a few big ones.
8.  Hand and discard pile: picks, copies and brings back its own cards, or plays what it draws.
9.  Strength or defence: its units lean to killing or to holding dots (defence holds a base); seen in the stats.
Dropped 9 Nov 2026: sets the pace or answers (Kaptain: its Runeterra order was wrong; most library games have no
answering on the enemy's turn, so there it only repeats removal); early or late (Kaptain: Claude could not measure it; checked against deck authors' words it was
right in only 63 of 149 Faeria deck pairs); moving (Kaptain: most library games have no moving); repair or prevent (Claude, after Kaptain's "get it
done": only places factions that keep units, so it becomes a monopoly choice of those factions); rows and areas (Claude,
same: "one cell or a row" repeats axis 6, "front or back rows" can't be measured from other games and stays a design
question per faction).
A game with under 5 cards for a spoke gives its factions no value on it, not 0. Gaps (9 Nov 2026): "one chosen" in every
game but Runeterra and Marvel Snap (the library rarely marks a chosen target); unit stats in Gwent, Stormbound, Into the
Breach and Slay the Spire; Into the Breach and Slay the Spire also on several others; "many at once" in Inscryption and
Monster Train; "units' own strikes" in Gwent.
How the axes were missed: the first measured axes came only from what cards do (concept shares), so identity that lives
in the cost curve, the stats and how a faction feels to play was invisible; look there too.
Which ends go together is judged by play: one end makes what the other needs, or undoes it (library fed/feeds/both).
A correlation across real factions only shows what those games happened to do, never that two ends can't work
(damaging or killing your own units to set off effects, locking enemies while moving, keeping a big unit alive by
repairing it: all sound plays).
Not axes: timing (players act now; round end is built FOR, not waited for), staying in one lane (every faction must
win 2 of 3 lanes), random effects (not allowed), lasting or this-round effects (this-round effects are worth little).

The measurement (9 Nov 2026): `library/cards_db/axes.py` measures the 49 real factions of the library (10+ cards each)
and writes `library/cards_db/axes.tsv` and `factions/wheel-axes.html` (circle, map of factions, numbers, closest factions;
template `tools/wheel_axes_template.html`). Each end of an axis is its own spoke (Kaptain, 9 Nov 2026: one number per axis,
one end minus the other, hid factions strong at both ends, e.g. Faeria Red has the most fixed damage and also strikes).
A faction's value on a spoke = the share of its cards that count for it, divided by its game's average: 0 = none,
1 = average. Which card effects count for each spoke is listed in axes.py and on hover in the page; each was checked
against the cards it catches, game by game, and some count only in some games (e.g. heal is the player's life in Faeria
and Slay the Spire, so it counts there not at all). Two counts: all cards, or cards weighted by how many decks of
decks.tsv play them (factions with fewer than 10 cards in decks get no deck count). Kaptain's points are checks in
axes.py (CHECKS); a failed one prints at every run.
The numbers quoted above came from an earlier chat with no saved script; this script gives Faeria Yellow 3.1 and Red 4.8
average cost as quoted, but Runeterra Shurima 3.0 / Freljord 4.3 (quoted 2.9 / 4.1) and Duelyst Lyonar 31% / Abyssian 43% strength (quoted 36% / 51%).
Lesson (9 Nov 2026, Kaptain found Faeria Red and Runeterra's pace wrong): Claude chose each axis end's concepts by their
names, without looking at which cards they catch in each game. Before an axis is used, an agent lists per game the cards
each of its concepts catches, and a concept stays only if those cards mean that end.
