Stage 1 has no cards yet. The example cards below only show the idea; they have no costs or stats.

THE THEME
The Glyphwrights are Daoist priests who write magic. What they are like:
- They write spells in ink on strips of paper (talismans) and stick them on people, doors and the dead. A talisman works
  as long as it stays stuck.
- A talisman can be burned to let all its power out at once.
- Every force has its opposite: yin and yang. Balance between them is strength; too much of one is weakness.
- Where a thing stands and which way it faces decides its luck (feng shui).
- Energy (qi) flows along lines and through things that touch, like water in channels.
- They make paper soldiers, servants and horses, and bring them to life.
- They copy the old texts with a brush and improve them; the written word is power.
- They brew elixirs for long life: slow, patient growth.
- They drive out spirits and seal them with a talisman.
- Everything changes into its opposite in turn (the I Ching).
- A master teaches, and the disciple carries the lesson on.
- They live in mountain hermitages, far behind the fighting.

THE FIVE MECHANICS

1. "I play my two kinds of card in turn -> each card played after the other kind is stronger."
   Every card of theirs is Yin (it protects, restores or binds; it works on your units) or Yang (it strikes, burns or
   drives out; it works on the enemy). A card played right after a card of the other kind gets its bonus. Two of the
   same kind in a row are weaker. So the player can't just play his best card; the order matters. The enemy answers by
   killing the unit a Yin card was meant for, which breaks the order.
   NEW RULE: a card's kind, and "your last card".
   Example card: a Yang spell that deals 2 damage, or 4 if your last card was Yin.
   Library: Slay the Spire's Watcher (going in and out of Calm and Wrath); Runeterra's Ionia ("if you've played 2+
   spells"). Alternating kinds is my idea.

2. "I stick a spell on a unit -> it keeps working, and later I burn it for a big effect."
   A talisman is a spell that stays on a unit (yours or the enemy's). While it stays, it works: a seal on an enemy
   holds it back, a ward on an ally protects it. Other cards burn talismans for a big effect at once. This is your idea
   of items: cards attached to units. The enemy answers by killing the unit that carries it.
   NEW RULE: spells attached to units.
   Example card: a seal talisman: the enemy unit carrying it can't attack; when it is burned, it deals 3 damage to that
   unit.
   Library: Runeterra's equipment (Bandle City, Noxus: The Papertree, Furious Wielder); Duelyst's Vetruvian
   attachments.

3. "My units are made of paper -> when they die they turn back into spells."
   Their units have low defence. Some spells make paper soldiers. A paper unit that dies puts a spell into your hand
   (your babushka idea). Burning a talisman can raise a paper soldier in its cell. So units and spells keep turning into
   each other. The enemy answers by killing them with plain numbers; they are weak to that.
   Example card: a paper soldier that, when it dies, puts a small Yang spell into your hand.
   Library: spells that make units and units that make spells (Duelyst's Songhai, Faeria, Runeterra).

4. "My units stand edge to edge -> what I cast on one flows through all of them."
   A Yin ward cast on one ally spreads to every ally touching it, 1 weaker at each step. A Yang strike on an enemy flows
   on to the enemies touching it (your chain lightning idea, damage that travels to edge-connected units). The player
   must keep his units packed, so they are easy to hit together. The enemy answers by killing the middle unit to break
   the line, and by keeping his own units apart.
   Example card: a spell that deals 3 damage to an enemy, then 2 to an enemy touching it, then 1 to the next.
   Library: chain damage in Monster Train and Runeterra; Duelyst's Magmar (cards that care about touching units). The
   ward that spreads to allies is my idea.

5. "I cast a weak spell -> it puts a better version of itself into my deck."
   The first copy of a spell is weak. Once cast, it shuffles an improved copy into the deck, like a disciple improving a
   master's text (your idea: spells that create more powerful spells in the deck). Their draw card is here: it draws one
   of your improved spells. The enemy answers by winning before the improved spells come.
   Example card: a small talisman that, once cast, shuffles a stronger talisman into your deck.
   Library: Runeterra's champion spells that shuffle stronger versions; Faeria's Hammer of Destruction (goes back into
   the deck).

HOW THE MECHANICS FEED EACH OTHER
- Talismans (2) and the improving spells (5) are the Yin and Yang cards (1).
- The bonus for the right order (1) makes the flow (4) reach further.
- Paper soldiers carry talismans (2) and connect the flow (4).
- Paper units die and come back as spells (3), which are cast and improve (5).
- Cards in two mechanics: a Yin talisman that wards a paper soldier and flows to its neighbours (2 and 4); a Yang
  spell that, cast after a Yin card, also raises a paper soldier (1 and 3).

WAYS TO PLAY THE FACTION
- Balance: perfect Yin, Yang, Yin, Yang order with small cards.
- Seals: talismans on enemy units that hold them back, burned later.
- Paper army: many cheap paper soldiers, and the spells their deaths give.
- Flow: a packed line of units where one spell hits or protects them all.

STRONG, A LITTLE, WEAK
- Strong at: spells (40-50% of the cards), creating cards, reaching the enemy back rows with spells, holding enemy
  units back with seals.
- Only a little: holding dots with bodies (paper soldiers do it badly), supply (Supply +1 disciples).
- Weak at: big units, defence, melee in the front row, changing lanes (one or two spells cast in another lane).
- Holding the lane's base: "As long as you hold this lane's base, my talismans can't be removed." "If you don't, burning
  me deals 2 more."
- How it should feel: like calligraphy: the right stroke after the right stroke.
