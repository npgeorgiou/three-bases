IRONFRONT — stage 1 (3 Nov 2026; Claude's design for Kaptain to check). Theme: World War I.

Stage 1 has no cards yet. The example cards below only show the idea; they have no costs or stats.

THE THEME
The Ironfront are the armies of World War I. What they are like:
- They dig trenches and wait in them for days under fire.
- The war is won by whoever can lose more men. The dead are replaced by the next men called up.
- Men in the front line wear down from mud and shell shock. They are sent back to rest and others take their place.
- Their guns stand far behind the front and fire over it, row by row.
- After the long wait, everyone climbs out of the trench at once on the whistle ("going over the top").
- Factories turn money into shells, and trains bring the shells to the front.
- The ground between the armies is deadly. Barbed wire and machine guns make the enemy pay for every step.
- Tanks are new, slow machines that break through a line.
- Sappers dig tunnels under the enemy and blow him up from below.
- Spotters in balloons tell the guns where to fire.
- The fallen get medals and memorials, and their names make the others braver.
- Little moves. Whoever breaks first loses.

THE FIVE MECHANICS

1. "My units don't move or attack this round -> at round end they gain strength."
   A unit that waits all round gains +1 strength at round end, and keeps it. Other cards spend that strength: one card
   makes every unit that waited strike at once (going over the top). Each turn the player chooses: act now, or wait and
   grow. The enemy answers by killing the waiting units before they grow, or by attacking them so they must answer.
   Example card: a rifleman who gains +1 strength at each round end in which he did not act.
   Library: Slay the Spire's Watcher (Calm stores energy, Wrath spends it); Runeterra's Blighted Battleaxe ("if my
   bearer didn't attack this round").

2. "My units die -> I get fresh units, supply or cards."
   Their front-row units are cheap and are meant to die. When one dies, something replaces it: supply for its lane, the
   next unit placed in that lane costs less, or a card is drawn ("the next men called up"). The player chooses which
   units to spend. The enemy answers by not killing them, or by killing the unit that gives the replacements.
   Example card: a conscript who, when he dies, makes the next unit you place in his lane cost 1 less.
   Library: Monster Train's Melting Remnant (units that burn out and come back); Stormbound's Swarm (value on death).

3. "My front row wears down -> my back rows recover and relieve them."
   Front-row units lose 1 shield at each round end (mud, shell shock); that is why they are cheap for their stats. Units
   in rows 2-3 restore their shields. Some cards swap a worn front unit with a fresh unit behind it, and both gain from
   the swap. This gives rows 2-3 a real job: the reserve. The enemy answers by hitting the reserve with Ranged units
   and spells.
   Example card: a stretcher bearer in row 2 who restores 1 shield to the unit in front of him at round end.
   Library: Duelyst's Vetruvian (cards that care about rows). The wear-down and rest is my idea.

4. "My guns stand in rows 2-3, where they can't fight in melee -> they hit the enemy's back rows."
   Guns fire into the enemy camp: some hit a whole enemy row, some hit the column a spotter in the front row points at.
   The few Projectiles of the faction belong here, in two kinds: shells that burst where they land, and mines that land,
   stay in the enemy camp, and go off when an enemy is placed next to them. The enemy answers with Ranged units and
   spells against the guns, or by keeping rows empty.
   Example card: a howitzer that deals damage to every enemy in one row of the enemy camp at round end.
   Library: Into the Breach's artillery; Into the Breach's Blobber, which throws a blob that stays and explodes later;
   your cannon idea.

5. "I let the enemy come into his front row -> every enemy that arrives there pays for it."
   An enemy unit that is placed or moved into the enemy front row, facing one of their machine-gun nests or wire, takes
   damage. The enemy loses units at the front, just as the Ironfront do (mechanic 2). The enemy answers by placing in
   other columns, or by fighting from his back rows with Ranged units.
   Example card: a machine-gun nest that deals 1 damage to each enemy placed in the cell facing it.
   Library: Into the Breach (damage when a unit enters a tile).

HOW THE MECHANICS FEED EACH OTHER
- The waiting units (1) need the guns (4) to do the fighting while they wait.
- The front row wears down (3) and dies (2); the dead come back as fresh units (2), who stand at the front (3) and
  face the enemy's arrivals (5).
- The guns (4) and the nests (5) weaken the enemy's front, so when the waiting units strike (1), they find weak targets.
- Cards in two mechanics: a rifleman who waits and grows (1) and gives supply when he dies (2); a howitzer that grows
  while it waits (1 and 4); a stretcher bearer who restores shields (3) and raises a medal when a unit dies (2).

WAYS TO PLAY THE FACTION
- Trench: units wait and grow, then all strike at once.
- Meat grinder: many cheap front units that die and are replaced, each death worth something.
- Big guns: the back rows do the killing; the front only holds the dots.
- Kill zone: nests and wire; the enemy loses units just by coming to the front.

STRONG, A LITTLE, WEAK
- Strong at: holding the front row at round end, long rounds, damage to the enemy's back rows.
- Only a little: movement (the relief swaps), drawing cards (the next men called up), tanks (a few big slow units).
- Weak at: a fast start, changing lanes, healing big units, and fighting every turn (units that act don't grow).
- Between lanes: trains that send supply or a unit to another lane, and one or two railway guns that fire into another
  lane.
- Holding the lane's base: "As long as you hold this lane's base, my units grow even if they moved." "If you don't,
  going over the top deals 1 more damage."
- How it should feel: the long wait under fire, then everything at once.
