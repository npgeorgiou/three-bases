NAME AND ICON
The Horologers. Icon: a clock face with two hands (new glyph "clockface"). Colour: brass on dark blue-grey.

THEME: the clockmakers' guilds of a 17th-18th century European clock city (the astronomical clocks of Prague and Strasbourg,
the automata of the Jaquet-Droz workshop, the Nuremberg guilds).
1. Their soldiers are automata: brass machines that do what they were built to do when the hour strikes.
2. A machine runs on a spring that must be wound; winding it releases its work at once.
3. Gears mesh: machines that touch drive each other, so one turn of a key moves a whole train.
4. The weights, pendulums and mainsprings hang at the back of every clock: the workings sit behind the face.
5. The great clock tower keeps the time of the whole city, every district at once.
6. Guild masters, journeymen and apprentices; apprentices oil, adjust and repair.
7. They prize precision and the schedule; they distrust improvisation.
8. Nothing is wasted: a broken machine is taken apart for spare parts, which go into the next one.
9. Brass and steel make the machines tough but clumsy: they rarely leave their place.
10. They live from commissions: patrons pay for pieces, and drawings (blueprints) are guarded secrets.
11. They fear rust and disorder: a broken chain stops the machine.
12. Their strength is repetition: the same motion, done again and again, without tiring.

IDENTITY (one line)
Machines that act by themselves at round end; the Horologers wind them so they act again during the round, and turn every
broken machine into spare parts that wind the next one.

MECHANICS (each "I do X -> I benefit from Y")
1. Striking the hour: my machines have round-end effects -> at round end they strike, deal damage, repair and build while
   the enemy can no longer answer. Most units carry one round-end effect (Ratchet Guard strikes the nearest enemy, Bell
   Ringer deals 1 damage to the enemy facing it). Library: turn_end (Inscryption Technology, Duelyst).
2. Winding: I wind a machine -> its round-end effect happens now, during the round, and I can wind the same machine again.
   Winding comes from spells (Turn the Key), from Place (the keyword Wound: the unit's round-end effect happens as it is
   placed), from the back rows (Mainspring winds the ally directly in front of it at round end) and from Spare Parts.
   Library: repeat_trigger (Faeria Imperial Engineer triggers all production abilities; Stormbound Doctor Mia triggers the
   bordering structures; Monster Train Transcendimp repeats Summon effects).
3. Gearing: my Geared machines touch each other -> winding one of them winds every Geared machine touching it, and those
   wind the ones touching them. A train of three Geared units turns one wind into three. Gearing only works on winds,
   never at the normal round end (where every machine acts anyway). Library: adjacency + repeat_trigger (Doctor Mia).
4. Salvage: my machines break -> each one with Salvage puts a Spare Part into my hand (a spell for 0: wind an ally and
   restore 1 of its shields). Parts are what makes winding cheap, and cards that "count your Spare Parts in hand"
   grow from them. Library: generate_cards on death (Monster Train Umbra), recycle.
5. The workings: my back-row machines drive the front -> rows 2-3 hold Mainsprings, Pendulums and Counterweights that
   wind, repair or strengthen the machine directly in front of them. This is the back rows' job. Claude's idea, built
   on Stormbound's "bordering" structures.

HOW THE MECHANICS FEED EACH OTHER
- Striking the hour gives Winding something to wind; Winding gives Striking the hour more strikes per round.
- Gearing multiplies Winding; a geared train is wound by one Spare Part.
- Salvage feeds Winding (Spare Parts wind) and is fed by machines that break themselves (Spent Spring, Clockwork Bomb:
  round end, deal damage, then I break).
- The workings feed Winding (back-row winders) and Striking the hour (Pendulums repair the front so it keeps acting).
MECHANIC Striking the hour | makes: round-end-effect, machine-breaks | needs: wind, repair
MECHANIC Winding | makes: wind | needs: round-end-effect, spare-part
MECHANIC Gearing | makes: wind, geared-train | needs: wind, adjacent-machines
MECHANIC Salvage | makes: spare-part | needs: machine-breaks
MECHANIC The workings | makes: wind, repair, adjacent-machines | needs: round-end-effect

KEYWORDS OF ITS OWN (hover texts in the game)
- Geared (value 0): when I am wound, every Geared ally touching me is wound too (each machine once per wind).
- Wound (value 1): Place: my round-end effects happen now.
- Salvage (value 1): Death: a Spare Part into your hand.
Shared keywords used: Immobile (a few heavy machines), Ranged (clock-tower guns), Armoured (a few brass guards).
Spare Part (token spell, cost 0): wind an ally and restore 1 of its shields.

GROUPS (ways to play; they mix)
1. The clock line: Ranged and round-end damage machines in rows 2-3, wound by spells; the front held by Armoured guards.
   Champion: The Great Clock Tower (round end: deals 1 damage to each enemy in its lane; links to the train group, because
   it is Geared).
2. The gear train: Geared units packed edge to edge in one lane, wound by Mainsprings and Spare Parts. Champion: The
   Grand Orrery (Geared; when wound, every ally in its lane gains +1 strength this round; links to the clock line).
3. The scrap yard: cheap Salvage machines that break themselves at round end, Spare Parts winding the rest, and units
   that grow with the Spare Parts in hand. Champion: Mistress of Salvage (round end: a Spare Part for each machine of
   yours that broke this round, up to 2; links to the gear train, since parts wind trains).
Two more champions: Master Horologer (Place: wind each ally in its row; anchors winding for every group) and Brass
Colossus (a 2x2 Armoured front machine with Salvage 2 effects that hold dots; links the scrap yard and the clock line).

STRONG / ONLY A LITTLE / WEAK
- Strong: damage at round end, many effects in one turn (a wound train), the back rows, staying power through repair.
- Only a little: drawing cards (Blueprints, Draughtsman), supply (Coal Boiler, Supply +1), between lanes (Clock Tower
  Bell, Pneumatic Post).
- Weak: moving (almost every machine stays where it is placed), answering the enemy's moves (no cards react to enemy
  placements or deaths), strong spells that remove (its spells wind, they rarely deal damage themselves).
- No card gives it reactions to enemy actions or Outrider-style movement.

INTERACTION WITH THE ENEMY: low to medium. It touches the enemy through round-end strikes and damage, never by reacting.

SELF-SUFFICIENCY
- Holds dots: Armoured Ratchet Guards, Brass Colossus, repaired front machines.
- Removes enemies: round-end strikes and damage, wound again during the round; Clock Tower Guns are Ranged.
- Supply: Coal Boiler (Supply +1), Furnace Engine (Supply +2, Immobile).
- Draw: Blueprints (spell: draw 2), Draughtsman (Place: draw 1 if you have 2 or more Geared units in its lane),
  Workshop Ledger (round end: draw 1 if you hold 2 or more Spare Parts; once per round, also when wound).
- Lane control: Guild Banner (has +2 strength while you hold this lane's base), Watchman (round end: if you don't hold
  this lane's base, deals 2 damage to the enemy facing it), Clock Keeper (both cases).
- Between lanes: Clock Tower Bell (when wound, winds one ally in each other lane), Pneumatic Post (spell: wind an ally
  in ANY lane; extremely rare kind of card).
- Hooks for two-faction decks: Turn the Key and Spare Parts wind any unit with a round-end effect (the Long Guns'
  Barrage, the Rootkin's Roots and Perennial, Exanimati round-end units).

ROWS 2-3: the workings (Mainspring, Pendulum, Counterweight) and the clock-tower guns.
DRAW, FREQUENCY: Blueprints once per card; Draughtsman once per card (Place); round-end draw always capped once per round,
also when it is wound (a wound round-end draw has lim round).
