==================================================================
THE FIVE NEW FACTIONS, FROM ZERO (3 Nov 2026; Claude's proposal, stage 1 for Kaptain to check)
==================================================================
Each faction has its own file in this folder: its theme, its five mechanics (each written as "I do X -> I benefit
from Y", with an example card and the library cards it comes from), how the mechanics feed each other, ways to play
it, and what it is strong and weak at. They replace the five factions in the game once each one is designed.

1. IRONFRONT (World War I), ironfront.txt. Slow; holds the front; its guns hit the enemy's back rows.
   - My units don't move or attack this round -> at round end they gain strength.
   - My units die -> I get fresh units, supply or cards.
   - My front row wears down -> my back rows recover and relieve them.
   - My guns stand in rows 2-3 -> they hit the enemy's back rows (the few Projectiles, of two kinds).
   - I let the enemy come into his front row -> every enemy that arrives there pays for it.

2. LODESTONES (the Stone Age builders of Stonehenge; all Immobile), lodestones.txt. Control and the late game.
   - My units can never move -> I drag enemy units where I want them.
   - I pack the enemy units together -> they crush each other (and a stone can topple onto them).
   - My stones stand in the same columns -> they share their power across lanes.
   - My stones are weak in the first rounds -> they grow every round they stand.
   - My stones fall -> the ground where they stood stays sacred.

3. GLYPHWRIGHTS (Daoist talisman masters), glyphwrights.txt. Spells and creating cards.
   - I play my two kinds of card in turn -> each card played after the other kind is stronger.
   - I stick a spell on a unit -> it keeps working, and later I burn it for a big effect.
   - My units are made of paper -> when they die they turn back into spells.
   - My units stand edge to edge -> what I cast on one flows through all of them.
   - I cast a weak spell -> it puts a better version of itself into my deck.

4. BROKERS (1920s gangsters), brokers.txt. Plays against the enemy the most.
   - I lend now, to myself or to the enemy -> I collect more later.
   - I let the enemy act -> his actions pay me.
   - I look at the enemy's cards -> I play them against him.
   - I turn one kind of wealth into another -> I always have what the board needs.
   - My units are cheap and weak alone -> the crew pays back every hit.

5. WILD HUNT (the ghostly hunt of the folk tales), hunters.txt. The fastest.
   - I choose one enemy as my prey -> everything I have hits it harder.
   - My hounds hit without killing -> my hunters gain against wounded enemies.
   - I kill -> my lane gets richer and the enemy's poorer.
   - I hold my hunters in hand and play several in one round -> they arrive as a pack.
   - My hunters fade if they don't kill -> they are stronger for their cost.

HOW THEY DIFFER: the Ironfront wait, the Hunt never waits. The Lodestones move the enemy; the Ironfront barely move. The
Glyphwrights fight with spells, the Hunt with strikes. The Brokers live off the enemy's actions; the Lodestones take
his choices away.
WHAT EACH ONE OWNS: moving enemy units (Lodestones), creating cards (Glyphwrights), seeing and stealing cards
(Brokers), supply taken from the enemy (Brokers and the Hunt), Mark (the Hunt), Projectiles (the Ironfront, a few
cards; other factions can use them).
FOR TWO-FACTION DECKS LATER: things each faction answers in its own way: a unit dies, a unit is moved, an enemy is
Bloodied, a spell is cast, an enemy acts.
NEW RULES THESE NEED (for Kaptain to decide): Mark; the two kinds of card and "your last card"; spells attached to
units; cards given to the enemy; marked cells; the Projectile keyword and its value.
