BROKERS — stage 1 (3 Nov 2026; Claude's design for Kaptain to check). Theme: the gangs of the 1920s Prohibition in

Stage 1 has no cards yet. The example cards below only show the idea; they have no costs or stats.

THE THEME
The Brokers are gangsters. What they are like:
- Protection rackets: shops pay them every week to be "left alone".
- They smuggle forbidden drink along hidden routes, from city to city.
- They run hidden bars behind ordinary shop fronts; the money comes in at night.
- They bribe police, judges and the rivals' men. Everyone has a price.
- They have informants: they know who said what, where the shipment is, what the rival plans.
- The loan shark gives money now and takes much more back later. Nobody escapes a debt.
- They launder money: dirty money is made clean through businesses; one kind of wealth becomes another.
- A contract on a man's name, and he disappears.
- Every street belongs to some gang.
- They run dice and rigged games; they always know the odds.
- The crew protects its own, and a traitor is punished.
- Their lawyers get them off for whatever they did.

THE FIVE MECHANICS

1. "I lend now, to myself or to the enemy -> I collect more later."
   A loan to yourself: you get supply or a card now, and at round end it is collected back, with interest from the
   enemy if one of your collectors stands in the lane, from you if not. A loan to the enemy: a card goes into the
   enemy's hand that gives him supply now, and at round end he pays you more than he got. The enemy answers by killing
   the collector, or by holding nothing to pay with.
   NEW RULE: cards given to the enemy.
   Example card: a loan shark who, when placed, gives the enemy 2 supply in this lane; at round end the enemy loses 3
   carry-over and you gain it.
   Library: Inscryption's Technology (cards that help the enemy); Stormbound's Shadowfen and Duelyst (units given to the
   enemy); the Hearthstone plague cards from Part 7.

2. "I let the enemy act -> his actions pay me."
   Each time an enemy unit standing facing one of their racketeers moves or attacks, or is placed there, you gain 1
   supply in that lane (once per enemy unit per round). Every action pays the same, whatever it costs the enemy, so
   the player chooses which enemy unit to stand in front of: the one that has to act. The enemy answers by killing the
   racketeer first, or by acting with units in other columns.
   Example card: a racketeer: when the enemy unit facing me attacks, you gain 1 supply.
   Library: none close; this comes from this game's rule that every action costs supply (my idea).

3. "I look at the enemy's cards -> I play them against him."
   Some cards show the enemy's hand or his next card. Other cards use what you saw: a contract that names a card in the
   enemy's hand, a copy of an enemy card that you can play. The enemy answers by playing out his hand and holding few
   cards.
   Example card: an informant who, when placed, shows you the enemy's hand and puts a copy of one of its cards into
   yours.
   Library: Runeterra's Bandle City and Shurima (seeing cards); Duelyst's Songhai; stealing that leads to a kill in
   Into the Breach, Runeterra and Stormbound.

4. "I turn one kind of wealth into another -> I always have what the board needs."
   A unit sold back to the hand gives supply; supply buys a card; a stolen card is paid for with the enemy's supply. It
   turns what the other mechanics bring in into what is needed now. The enemy can't stop it directly, but every step
   is an action he can answer.
   Example card: a front business: when placed, return one of your units to your hand and gain supply equal to its
   cost.
   Library: Inscryption (one resource turned into another); Faeria's Pandora; Monster Train's shop gold.

5. "My units are cheap and weak alone -> the crew pays back every hit."
   When a crew unit is struck, another unit of the crew strikes back at the attacker. When a crew unit dies, its killer
   gets a contract on him (a mark for the crew's revenge). The enemy pays for every hit he makes. The enemy answers with
   damage to many units at once, and by killing the strongest first.
   Example card: an enforcer: when an ally next to me is struck, I strike the attacker.
   Library: Duelyst's Lyonar and Runeterra's Demacia ("when an ally is struck"); marks in Monster Train's Stygian Guard.

HOW THE MECHANICS FEED EACH OTHER
- The rackets (2) and the loans (1) bring supply, which pays for looking at cards (3) and turning wealth around (4).
- A card you gave the enemy is a card you know he holds (1 and 3).
- Stolen cards are sold or played (4).
- The crew (5) are the racketeers and collectors, and every hit on them makes the enemy act next to them (2).
- Cards in two mechanics: a racketeer who taxes the facing enemy (2) and, sold back to the hand, gives what he took as
  supply (4); a loan card (1) that also shows you the enemy's hand (3).

WAYS TO PLAY THE FACTION
- Racket: many racketeers; the enemy's own actions pay for everything.
- Loan shark: gifts that the enemy pays back double.
- Informants: steal the enemy's best cards and play them.
- The crew: cheap units that punish every hit.

STRONG, A LITTLE, WEAK
- Strong at: playing against the enemy (the most of all five), stealing and seeing cards, supply taken from the enemy.
- Only a little: damage (a few contracts and hits), defence (the crew).
- Weak at: big units, plain damage, holding a lane by force, damage to many units at once, healing.
- Between lanes: smuggling: supply taken in one lane delivered to another (a few cards); one gangster with Outrider
  (the getaway car).
- Holding the lane's base: "As long as you hold this lane's base, my racket takes 2." "If you don't, my loans cost the
  enemy 1 more."
- How it should feel: the enemy pays for everything he does.
