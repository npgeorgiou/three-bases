WILD HUNT — stage 1 (3 Nov 2026; Claude's design for Kaptain to check). Theme: the Wild Hunt of northern European
folk tales (Odin's or Herne's ghostly hunt riding the storm with hounds and horns), and real medieval hunting.
==================================================================
Stage 1 has no cards yet. The example cards below only show the idea; they have no costs or stats.

THE THEME
The Wild Hunt are ghostly huntsmen. What they are like:
- Once they have the scent, they never stop.
- The huntsman chooses one animal, and the whole hunt goes after it.
- Packs of hounds wear the prey down before the hunters arrive.
- A horn call brings the whole hunt at once.
- A wounded animal leaves a blood trail; it is easy to follow and easy to finish.
- They take trophies: antlers, skins, heads. A hunter's standing grows with each kill.
- They ride across the sky in the storm, from place to place in one night.
- Villagers lock their doors in fear; whoever sees the Hunt is swept along or flees.
- A great huntsman leads, and everyone follows his choice.
- The Hunt must keep killing; if it stops, it fades away.
- They are strongest at night, when the prey can't see them.
- The kill is shared at a feast, and the pack grows.

THE FIVE MECHANICS

1. "I choose one enemy as my prey -> everything I have hits it harder."
   A card marks one enemy unit (one mark per lane at a time). Strikes on the marked enemy deal double (your Mark idea),
   and many cards gain while the prey lives, or when it dies. The player chooses which enemy is worth the whole hunt.
   The enemy answers by protecting it, moving it away, or letting it die cheaply.
   NEW RULE: Mark.
   Example card: a huntsman who, when placed, marks the enemy facing him.
   Library: marks in Monster Train's Stygian Guard, Runeterra's Freljord and Noxus; Duelyst's Grasp of Agony.

2. "My hounds hit without killing -> my hunters gain against wounded enemies."
   Their hounds have low strength and strike often, so the enemy is left Bloodied (less defence than its card shows).
   Other cards gain from Bloodied enemies: they finish them for a bonus, gain strength against them, or reach them from
   the back rows. The enemy answers by restoring shields (that ends Bloodied), or with Armoured units.
   Example card: a finisher who gains +2 strength when he attacks a Bloodied enemy.
   Library: damage, then a check on the target's health, then a kill (Duelyst, Monster Train, Runeterra); Runeterra's
   Noxus (cards that care about damaged enemies).

3. "I kill -> my lane gets richer and the enemy's poorer."
   Each kill makes the hunter's lane give more supply, for good (your idea: units that permanently gain their own
   supply on a kill), while the enemy's lane loses the dead unit's supply. Their draw card is here: the feast, which
   draws a card when the prey dies (once per round). They have no other way to get rich, so they must keep killing.
   The enemy answers by giving them nothing to kill: few, big units.
   Example card: a trophy hunter who gains Supply +1 each time his strike kills.
   Library: units that grow on a kill (Duelyst, Runeterra); Inscryption's Beast (bones from deaths); Monster Train
   (resources on a kill).

4. "I hold my hunters in hand and play several in one round -> they arrive as a pack."
   Horn cards and Place effects reward several hunters arriving in the same lane in one round: each one placed after
   the first gains. On the horn, riders in the back rows move up and attack in one go (your idea: Charge units in back
   rows that move and attack together, paying both). The enemy answers by killing the first arrivals, or by keeping his
   front row full so the riders have no target.
   Example card: a horn spell: the hunters you place in this lane this round gain +1|+1.
   Library: Duelyst's Lyonar (units entering play); Monster Train's Hellhorned; Faeria's Yellow (charging units).

5. "My hunters fade if they don't kill -> they are stronger for their cost."
   A hungry hunter loses 1 shield at each round end in which no enemy died in his lane. That drawback buys him stats
   above the curve (the rules on drawbacks). It pushes the faction to attack every round, and it makes the enemy's
   choice to hide matter. The enemy answers by hiding and trading slowly.
   Example card: a hungry hound, strong for its cost, that loses 1 shield at each round end without a kill in its lane.
   Library: Monster Train's Melting Remnant (Burnout); Runeterra's Shadow Isles (units that fade).

HOW THE MECHANICS FEED EACH OTHER
- The horn brings many hunters (4), whose many small strikes leave enemies Bloodied (2).
- The prey takes double (1) and dies.
- Kills pay trophies (3), which pay for the next horn (4) and keep the hungry hunters from fading (5).
- Cards in two mechanics: a hound that leaves enemies Bloodied (2) and gains from the horn (4); a huntsman who marks
  when placed (1) and takes a trophy when he kills (3).

WAYS TO PLAY THE FACTION
- The prey: one mark, everything goes at it.
- Hounds: many cheap strikes, then the finishers.
- The ride: back-row riders charging in on the horn.
- Feast: kill for supply and out-earn the enemy.

STRONG, A LITTLE, WEAK
- Strong at: early pressure, killing weak and wounded units, supply from kills.
- Only a little: changing lanes (riding the storm: a few riders go to the lane the prey fled to; Outrider on very few),
  spells (about 25%: horns, marks, the feast).
- Weak at: defence, the late game, holding a lane after losing a fight, big units, damage to many units at once,
  cards that work without killing.
- Holding the lane's base: "As long as you hold this lane's base, my trophies give 1 more." "If you don't, I'm
  hungrier: +1 strength."
- How it should feel: the chase: choose the prey, run it down, feast, choose again.
