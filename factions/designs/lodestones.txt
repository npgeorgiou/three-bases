Stage 1 has no cards yet. The example cards below only show the idea; they have no costs or stats.

THE THEME
The Lodestones are the people who raised the great standing stones. What they are like:
- Once a stone is raised, it never moves again. It stands for thousands of years.
- A whole village hauls one stone, on ropes and rollers.
- So they know how to drag great weights wherever they want them.
- They set stones in long straight lines (Carnac). A stone in line with another means something.
- The stones are built to the sun and the stars; they are a calendar. The longer the year turns, the more they mean.
- The dead are laid in stone tombs, and the stones hold their spirits.
- Stone circles close off sacred ground. What is inside belongs to them.
- Nothing is hurried. They win by outlasting.
- At midsummer the tribes gather at the stones for feasts.
- A standing stone claims the land around it.
- A falling stone crushes what is under it.
- They make a few great works, not many small ones.

THE FIVE MECHANICS

1. "My units can never move -> I drag enemy units where I want them."
   Their cards move enemy units: drag one forward toward the base, pull one into the column of a stone, swap two enemy
   units. The player chooses where to put each enemy so the other mechanics hit it. The enemy answers with units that
   can't be moved, or by leaving no free cells to be dragged into.
   Example card: a hauling stone that, when placed, drags the enemy unit in its column forward as far as it can go.
   Library: forced-movement chains that end in a kill (Duelyst, Into the Breach, Runeterra, Stormbound).

2. "I pack the enemy units together -> they crush each other."
   An enemy unit dragged into another unit, or against the edge where it can't go further, takes damage, and so does
   the unit it hit. Also, a stone can be thrown down on purpose onto the enemy units facing it, dealing damage equal to
   its defence: the player loses the stone and the enemy takes the blow. Grown stones (mechanic 4) fall harder. The
   enemy answers by spreading out, which leaves dots open.
   Example card: a spell that topples one of your stones onto the enemy facing it.
   Library: Into the Breach (units pushed into other units take damage; Force Amp, "+1 damage from bumps").

3. "My stones stand in the same columns -> they share their power across lanes."
   Stones in the same column in two or three lanes help each other: what one gains, the others gain, and their
   round-end damage is shared. Enemy units standing in a stone's column are hit by it at round end. This is the
   faction's job between lanes. The enemy knows where the stones stand and tries to keep out of those columns, so the
   dragging (mechanic 1) puts them back.
   Example card: a menhir that deals 1 damage at round end to the enemy facing it, and to the enemies facing each
   stone of yours in the same column in the other lanes.
   Library: Duelyst's Vetruvian obelisks. Sharing across lanes is my idea.

4. "My stones are weak in the first rounds -> they grow every round they stand."
   Every stone gains a little at each round end it survives (+1 defence or +1 strength, and keeps it). Some cards get a
   bonus from round 4 on. Each round the game gets worse for the enemy. The enemy answers by winning early, or by
   killing stones before they grow.
   Example card: an old stone that gains +0|+1 at each round end.
   Library: Inscryption's Giant ("+1 power every turn"); turn-start growth in Duelyst and Stormbound.

5. "My stones fall -> the ground where they stood stays sacred."
   When a stone dies, its cell stays marked. An enemy unit placed or dragged into a marked cell takes damage, and the
   next stone raised on that cell costs less. The enemy answers by killing stones where it costs him least.
   NEW RULE: marked cells.
   Example card: a burial mound that, when it dies, marks its cell.
   Library: Duelyst's Abyssian Shadow Creep (cells that damage). The cheaper stone is my idea.

HOW THE MECHANICS FEED EACH OTHER
- Dragging (1) packs the enemy (2) and puts enemies in a stone's column (3).
- Crushed and lined-up enemies die, so the stones stand longer and grow (4).
- Grown stones topple harder (2). A toppled stone leaves sacred ground (5), and the enemy is dragged onto it (1).
- Cards in two mechanics: a stone that drags an enemy when placed (1) and hits its column at round end (3); a tomb
  that grows each round (4) and leaves sacred ground when it dies (5).

WAYS TO PLAY THE FACTION
- Hauling: drag and crush the enemy; the most interaction.
- The long line: stones in the same column in all three lanes.
- Henge: a few huge stones that grow and outlast the enemy.
- Barrow ground: stones that fall on purpose and come back cheaper on sacred cells.

STRONG, A LITTLE, WEAK
- Strong at: deciding where enemy units stand, round-end damage, the late game, acting between lanes.
- Only a little: spells (about 20%: hauling and midsummer), supply (plain Supply +1 workers).
- Weak at: moving or changing lanes, drawing cards (one card: the oracle stone), a fast start, many small units,
  reaching enemies that are not in a stone's column.
- Holding the lane's base: "As long as you hold this lane's base, the enemies I drag take 1 damage." "If you don't, I
  drag twice."
- How it should feel: the enemy army slowly pulled onto the stones.
