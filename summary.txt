THREE BASES — DESIGN DOCUMENT
Turn-based computer card/board game in the browser, delivered as a single HTML file. This document is the game as it is now: rules, all current cards, UI and the AI lab. No plans or ideas (factions.txt) and no open work (todo.md).

==================================================================
1. FILES
==================================================================
- three-bases-game.html — the game. Factions: Tagma, Ashfang Horde, Exanimati. Playable against the Benchmark or the Random player, or by two players on the same screen.
- three-bases-scenarios.html — ready boards to try the rules: the game plus a scenario page (each board starts a test game with unlimited supply and an opponent that only passes; a panel over the opponent's deck explains what to do and what to watch, with Reset and All boards). Built from the game file plus a scenario script and its styles appended at the end. Boards: a charge into two units, Pyre, Frenzy retargeting, a Powder Keg chain, Mass Grave, Danse Macabre (the fallen rise), Debt of the Fallen (the cost falls), Tomb Warden (lasting gains come back), Undying Reaver (back to the hand with its changes), Stampede, a Revenant returning inside a chain, Exanimati death reactions, placing/dragging/picking, and a big chain.
- three-bases-ai-lab.html — the AI lab (section 16). It runs on an earlier card list from before the current factions.
- library/ — the inspiration library (see library/README.md).
- factions.txt — ideas for factions and cards; todo.md — work not done yet; CLAUDE.md — how Claude works on these files.
Tags in this document: AGREED = decided by Kaptain; "proposed" / "Claude's suggestion" = not confirmed yet.

==================================================================
2. CODE STRUCTURE
==================================================================
- Build UI behaviour once, generically (one marker layer, previews played on a copy of the game, one hand layout), never as per-card patches.
- Code structure (October 2026): the engine resolves effects through one resolution queue (section 7, resolution chains). The game file has an engine layer (rules, state, actions; no screen code) and a screen layer that reads the state and replays the engine's events. Each ability declares its scope once in the engine: its area (cells) and whom it affects (the SCOPE table for unit abilities, SPELL_SCOPE for spells). The rules resolve abilities on those targets, and the screen highlights the same area and units. Legal targets and move cells come from the engine's list of legal actions; previews and kill marks come from playing the action on a copy of the game. A new card needs a scope entry and its rule, never screen code.

==================================================================
3. BOARD AND BASES
==================================================================
- 3 lanes. Each lane: my camp (bottom) and the enemy camp (top), facing a base in the middle.
- Camp grid: 3 columns × 3 rows. The row nearest the base is the front row.
- No hits between lanes. Lane switching only via special moves (e.g. Outrider).
- Cells occupied by one unit are drawn as one merged cell.
- Each base has 3 dots, one in front of each front-row column. A unit of yours in that front-row cell gives you the dot; if the opponent also has a unit in its facing front-row cell, the dot is cancelled. 2 or more dots = the base is yours.
- No base powers in the game yet.

==================================================================
4. WINNING AND LOSING
==================================================================
- Hold 2 or more bases at the end of a round that you also held when that round started (ownership may change during the round; only start and end count). Nobody holds a base when round 1 starts, so the earliest win is at the end of round 2.
- If a round ends with a player having no carry-over and no units that generate supply, that player loses. If both do, both lose.
- If three rounds in a row pass without either player taking any action, for whatever reason (passing by choice or having nothing legal to do), both players lose. The first two such rounds are free, so carry-over can be saved; the count resets the moment either side does anything.
- Withdrawing (AGREED, 10 Oct 2026) is a loss: the other side wins, and the end popup says who withdrew.

==================================================================
5. ROUNDS, TURNS AND SUPPLY
==================================================================
- Before the game, each player sees their starting hand and can redraw up to 3 cards, each dealt card at most once. When the battle begins, both players learn how many cards the other redrew (not which).
- The game starts with 4 carry-over supply for each player.
- The game is played in rounds. Each action (deploy, move, attack, play a spell) is one turn; then the other player acts. Moving and attacking stay separate actions, each its own turn (AGREED, 10 Oct 2026).
- A player can PASS instead of acting (Space = Pass). When both players pass in a row, the round ends. If a player can't take any action, they pass automatically.
- Who acts first in round 1 is random (AGREED, October 2026); after that the starting player alternates each round.
- Round start: each player gets, per lane, supply equal to their units' supply there; each player draws 2 cards (not in round 1). Drawing from an empty deck draws nothing.
- A hand holds at most 10 cards; with 10 in hand you draw nothing.
- Supply of a lane is spent only in that lane; carry-over can be used in any lane. Payment uses the lane first, then carry-over.
- Deploying, moving and attacking each cost the unit's supply.
- Each unit can move once and attack once per round, unless its card says otherwise (e.g. Lightfooted, Flurry).
- Movement goes to any free spot in your own camp, in any orientation; rotating in place also counts as a move. Units with a shape of their own (Link merges, spread or eaten cells, the grown Stitching Table) are single units of that shape: they move and turn in quarter turns like any unit. A turn animates as one motion (turning while travelling), never a move followed by a turn.
- Round end: round-end abilities fire, then leftover supply fills the carry-over lane by lane from the owner's left to right (mirrored for the opponent) until it holds 5 (trial value; was 3).

==================================================================
6. UNITS — GENERAL RULES
==================================================================
- Shapes: 1 cell, line of 2, line of 3, 2x2 square. Units can take any orientation. Some Exanimati units and tokens can be amorphous (any connected shape).
- Stats: strength, defence, supply. Supply is both what the unit gives its lane each round and what it costs (to deploy and per action). It represents the resources the unit can muster.
- Supply ranges from 1 upward; costs above 6 are allowed (the supply-to-stats rule continues at 2 per supply). Size is independent of supply but eats into the stat budget (section 9).
- Minimum printed defence is 1 (0 defence means dead). Values never go negative. Units can gain lasting strength and defence.
- Units with 0 strength can't attack.
- Damage breaks shields; if the unit survives, it keeps fewer shields from then on.
- Reach (AGREED, October 2026): a melee unit reaches its adjacent area: every cell around its cells, diagonals included. For this, the base doesn't exist: the two front rows of a lane touch, so a unit in your front row also reaches the enemy front-row cells facing its cells and the ones diagonally next to them (a unit in your front-right cell reaches the enemy's middle and right front-row cells; a unit in the front-middle cell reaches all three). A Pike unit also reaches the enemy front-row cells two rows ahead of it (column ±1), so from your second row it reaches the enemy front row the same way. A unit in the enemy camp (thrown, defected, resurrected there) reaches the cells around it in that camp. Ranged units reach every cell of their lane. Multi-cell units reach around every cell they cover. Attacks hit only enemies in reach.
- Strike and damage (AGREED, October 2026): a strike is a hit with the unit's strength: its attacks and charges, Riposte, a thrown unit landing on a unit, and the strikes with its strength that abilities and spells make it deal (Manganon, Frenzy, Martyrdom, Monomachia, Stampede). Strikes reach only what the unit could attack (reach above); with several enemies in reach, it strikes the nearest one (AGREED, 10 Oct 2026): the shortest distance between their cells, the two camps of a lane counting as one column of rows, so the enemy directly facing it across the base comes before the diagonal ones, and for ranged and Pike strikes nearer rows come first; equally near enemies go in resolution order (section 7). A fixed amount from a unit's ability, or from a spell, is damage dealt, not a strike: it keeps its own target rule (e.g. the nearest enemy in the unit's column, however deep), and effects that fire on strikes (Gorehound, Duelist, Leper Knight, Plague Slinger, Blighted Shepherd, Executioner, Headsman, Plunderer, Trophy Taker) don't fire on it.
- No active (triggered) abilities on units (no Rally, Blood Price). Outrider is a move, not an active ability, and is fine. No ranged units that attack only under conditions.
- No timing/build-up cards (e.g. "at 3 counters, do X"). Round-end abilities are wanted, as are cards that play on positioning relative to other units (adjacency, rows, formations).

==================================================================
7. AREAS, POSITIONS AND RESOLUTION
==================================================================
- The only areas are row, column and whole camp. An area starts from the cells the unit itself occupies, so a multi-cell unit covers every row or column it stands in.
- "In front" and "behind" are measured relative to the enemy camp, so orientation matters: a vertical 2-cell unit in the first two cells of a column has only the back-row cell behind it.
- Facing: units in the same column of the two camps of a lane face each other. "Directly in front / behind / beside" = the next cell in that direction.
- Facing / nearest enemy in its column: the enemy nearest to the base in the unit's own column(s) of the enemy camp (front row first, then deeper rows, left to right from your side); nothing if that column is empty. Damage that abilities deal uses it; strikes use reach instead (section 6).
- Adjacent = the 3x3 square around the unit (diagonals included), clipped to its camp.
- Order of events on placement: the placed unit's card text, then its keywords in order, then the next unit's text and keywords, and so on.
- Gains are instant, actions take turns (AGREED, 10 Oct 2026): when something happens (a death, a return, a move), every unit that only gains from it gains in that same instant (Carrion Chief, Pallid Mourner, Grafter, Ossuary Bellringer's supply, Tomb Warden's +1|+1 on a return, Tourmarches' +1|+1 as the ally sets off); gains caused by gains are instant too. Then the effects that act on other units (damage, strikes, drains, Plague, draws) take turns as below, each with its own chain.
- Order of reactions (AGREED): when something happens to a unit (placed, returns, dies), after the instant gains, the unit's own effects fire first, then the reactions of the units in its own camp (reading order), then those of the enemy camp (reading order). E.g. a returning Mardoc strikes with its return effect before enemy Akritai react to it; when a unit dies, its Death effects and returns come first, then its allies' reactions (Frenzy, Strix, Carrion Chief on its side…), then the enemy's (Carrion Chief on their side, Mother Ophra…).
- Resolution chains (AGREED, 10 Oct 2026; replaces the earlier resolution steps): an effect resolves in steps, in the order its text gives, and each step picks its targets at the moment it happens (never in advance). An effect that happens all at once (a charge into two units, Pyre, Rain of Blood, Greek Fire, Firestorm, Powder Keg's blast, Bile Bloater's Death damage, Monomachia, Stampede, the Mass Grave fliers reaching enemies at the same moment) hits all its targets at the same moment, and the units it kills die at that moment: they can't act or react, hold no dots, and their tombstones keep their cells. Then the affected units take turns in resolution order. Each one opens its chain: what the hit does to it, its death if it died (its Death effects, its camp's reactions, the enemy's), and every reaction these trigger; each effect inside a chain opens its own chain for the units it hits, which closes before that chain continues. When a dead unit's chain is closed, it leaves the board; only then can a Shade or a returning Revenant take its cells (its own return or Shade comes during its chain). Example: a Bile Bloater and a Flesh Shield die in the same Pyre; the Bile Bloater resolves first and its 1 damage kills the enemy in front of the Flesh Shield; then the Flesh Shield's 2 damage goes to the nearest enemy now in its column. Other multi-step effects: an attack (Gold Grubber's loot, then the strike, its deaths, then Reckless), Riposte (after the strike's deaths), Charge (the move, then the strike, then Reckless), each Place effect, Cinder Imp (the ally, then the enemy), Blood Sorcerer (the allies, then the enemy), Goblin Sapper, Pit Fighter, a throw, Onslaught (unit by unit), Danse Macabre (the fallen rise one by one), and each round-end ability.
- Simultaneous effects (AGREED): at round end, the round starter's effects resolve first, then the opponent's.
- Resolution order (AGREED; corrected 10 Oct 2026): the first row is the row closest to the base. From the seat of the player who owns the effect: the front rows first (each camp's own front row), then the middle rows, then the back rows; at the same depth, lanes and then columns left to right as that player sees them, the owner's camp before the enemy's. So the first enemy a strike finds is in the enemy front row. The opponent's effects follow the same order from the opponent's seat.
- Spreading units (AGREED): spread first into the unit's own row in reading order, then the row behind it in reading order, then further back if possible; once every row behind is covered, they move to the rows in front. A new cell must share an edge with the mass (no diagonal or detached cells). Spreading stays in the owner's camp; it never enters the enemy camp.

==================================================================
8. RULINGS
==================================================================
- Strike and damage (AGREED, October 2026; replaces the earlier ruling): card texts write "strike" only for hits with the unit's strength (section 6) and "deals N damage" for fixed amounts from abilities and spells; "damage" alone covers both (e.g. "survives damage").
- Kinds of stat change and their wording (AGREED, 10 Oct 2026): a lasting change uses a plain verb with no time word ("gains +1 strength", "loses 2 strength"); it stays with the unit until it leaves the board (and through Revenant returns and returns to the hand, below). A change for this round only ends with "this round" ("gains +1|+1 this round"; replaces "until round end"). A position bonus says "have" ("Other allies in my row have +1 strength"): it holds only while its condition holds (auras, Shield wall, Pack, Skirmisher). Lost shields are separate: they stay lost until restored. The word "permanently" is not used.
- Place (keyword and event) (AGREED): a unit is placed every time it enters the grid — when played from the hand, when it defects, when it is resurrected, when a Revenant returns, and when a token appears. Place effects (Charge, Mandator, Monomachos, Komes, Presbeutes, Crowd Pleaser, Cinder Imp, and the Voukinator/Warhorn bonuses for units placed later) fire on every placement, so a defected unit fires its Place effects for its new side. A Place choice can only be made by its owner on their own turn; a unit placed at any other time (during the opponent's turn or the round end) skips the choice (Cinder Imp then makes only its enemy strike).
- Follow-up choices: when a card needs a choice after it is played (a Place pick, Reform Ranks' moves, a defector's new spot, Monomachia's enemy, Synaspismos' partner), the same player keeps the turn until the choice is made; the turn then passes. Mandator and Reform Ranks may be finished early (Done); the other choices are required.
- An effect with no free target cell (push, pull, defection) does nothing.
- Armoured blocks the first damage each round from any source, spells and own strikes included. Damage blocked by armour still counts as surviving damage (Varangoi, Pain Eater).
- Charge (AGREED, 10 Oct 2026): a unit placed in its own front row doesn't charge (it has no run-up), so a Warhorn unit placed there neither charges nor dies from Reckless. Charge strikes only if the unit reaches its front row, and then every enemy in the facing front-row cells of its columns (not the diagonal ones), each with its full strength (a 2-wide charger can strike two units); a charger stopped short strikes no one. A 0-strength charger that reaches enemies still counts as striking them for the charger only (Reckless fires, so e.g. a Warhorn-charged Powder Keg dies and explodes); the strike does nothing to the target, uses up no Armour and doesn't count as surviving damage. Logged, no animation.
- Ability damage (Akritai, Cinder Imp, Blood Drum, Blood Sorcerer, Powder Keg, Flesh Shield, Pyre Colossus) adds the unit's current strength bonuses, on every target (own units included). Every damage amount (abilities, charges, attacks) is fixed when the effect starts and stays the same for all its strikes, even if the unit gains or loses strength meanwhile (e.g. a Cinder Imp boosted to 3 that kills the Blood Drum still strikes the enemy with 3); for Death effects, the bonuses it had when it died. A unit's card (hover) shows these numbers with its bonus included, highlighted in a small dark chip; Blood Sorcerer's bonus reads "…1 for each ally struck, plus N".
- Losing a defence bonus (an aura such as Strategos or Shield wall ends, or a bonus "this round" such as Tourmarches' expires): the unit keeps its current defence if that is at or below its defence without the bonus; otherwise its defence drops to that value. So a unit that lost shields while boosted loses nothing more when the boost ends (as in Legends of Runeterra).
- Pull (Monomachos): the enemy moves straight forward in its column, toward its base, as far as the cells are free. Monomachos' Place pick (AGREED, October 2026) offers only enemies that can move at least one cell forward; with none, there is no pick.
- Defect: the unit is placed with the new side in a free spot of that side's camp in the same lane and can't act that round.
- Akritai react to any arrival into an enemy front-row cell facing them: placement, moves, charges, pulls, Onslaught, defection, returns. A placed unit's own effects fire first (its Place effects, after its Place choice once made, and a Revenant's return effect), then the Akritai react (a Plague Acolyte gives Plague before it is struck).
- Warhorn: units placed later gain Charge and Reckless; the charge strike counts as their attack, so they die after it; with nobody to strike, or placed in the front row, they survive.
- Cinder Imp: if it has no other unit in its lane, only the enemy strike happens.
- Rock Hurler's splash: the cell behind the target gets half its strength, the one after a quarter, rounded down.
- Thousand Cuts may also target your own units; Armoured blocks only the first of its damage.
- Riposte (AGREED) answers attacks and charge strikes, not strikes from spells such as Stampede.
- Monomachia offers only units with strength. Bodyguards also protect each other.
- Varangian Guard (AGREED, October 2026): it gains its +1 defence after taking the damage for the ally, if it survives.
- Resurrection (AGREED): the killed unit comes back on your side as the same card, with debuffs that halve its printed strength, defence and supply (rounded down, no minimum; a unit whose defence would halve to 0 can't come back and stays dead). A unit resurrected from the discard pile (Raise the Fallen, Grave Robber, Aurelius, Danse Macabre, Katabasis) comes back fresh: none of its earlier gains, losses or damage. It is placed (Place effects fire), then auras and other area effects apply to it again. With a Banner of the Returned in its lane, a returning Revenant keeps its printed strength; its defence and supply still halve (AGREED, October 2026).
- Revenants keep their lasting changes (AGREED, 10 Oct 2026): a returning Revenant keeps its lasting stat gains and losses and its gained or lost keywords (e.g. Komes' +1 defence, Tomb Warden's +1|+1, Creeping Rot's -2 strength, a Revenant given by Embalmer); it doesn't keep lost shields or anything "this round". How many times it returns is still decided only by halving the printed stats. Example: a Knight of the Ninth Barrow (3|3) with Komes' +1 defence returns as 1|1 plus +0|+1, and Tomb Warden adds +1|+1: 2|3. On its next death its printed defence 1 would halve to 0, so it stays dead.
- Returning to the hand with changes (AGREED, 10 Oct 2026): a unit that goes back to the hand (Undying Reaver) keeps its lasting stat changes and its gained or lost keywords; lost shields aren't kept. Its hand card shows the changed strength and defence in green (higher) or red (lower) and adds a line "Kept from the battlefield: …" listing them; played again, the unit comes back with them. Built as a general mechanism (any future card that returns a unit to the hand works the same way); in the discard pile the card is always the plain deck card.
- One return per death (AGREED, 10 Oct 2026): a dead unit's card can go back only once per death, and the first effect that takes it from the discard pile wins. Revenant goes first, so an embalmed Undying Reaver returns to the board (halved) until its defence would halve to 0; then its card is still in the discard pile and its own Death effect takes it back to the hand (with Revenant kept). Tokens have no card in the discard pile, so an embalmed token still returns.
- The discard pile is the graveyard. Tokens vanish when they die (no card goes to the discard pile).
- Units you control in the enemy camp (AGREED): they count for your base dots, give you their supply, and you select and use them like any of your units. Positions are measured (AGREED) from the camp the unit stands in, so it moves within that camp, its "front row", "facing" and melee attacks point at the opposite camp, and it strikes or affects only enemies. Such a unit has a ring in its controller's colour (green you, red opponent). When it dies, its card goes to its controller's discard pile.
- Built choices (AGREED, 10 Oct 2026): Shades have supply 1; Gorvan rises as soon as all four parts are done; Hungering Mass takes the eaten unit's cells; the Ogre Thrower's throw costs what an attack costs.

==================================================================
9. CARD DESIGN RULES
==================================================================
- Costing check (AGREED, October 2026; Claude applies it to every card it designs or costs): (1) play the card out over a whole game, in the deck built for it; (2) find what limits the mechanic it touches: a card that removes the only limit on a repeating effect (returns, Place, round end) is near-unbounded and is never costed as a small bonus; (3) compare it with the strongest card at the same cost. Defence is what holds the dots, so defence gains, and defence that keeps coming back, are worth more than strength.
- Supply-to-stats rule (AGREED): total strength + defence of a vanilla unit by supply: 1→3, 2→5, 3→7, 4→8, 5→10, then 2 per supply (6→12, 7→14, 8→16, 9→18, 10→20). Up to 5 it follows round(3 × S^0.75); from 5 on it is 2 × S. Size takes its share (line of 2: 1, line of 3: 2, 2x2: 3), and the ability gets the rest.
- Rationale: 2#3|3 or 3#1|8 are too strong early/mid game. Examples: 5#5|5, 10#10|10.
- Keyword values (AGREED): the values in section 11 "Keywords by faction".
- Card text (AGREED): written ability text, beyond its keywords and agreed values such as Supply N, costs at least 1 point; abilities worth more cost what their effect is worth (Claude's judgement, open to change, following the costing check in section 2). E.g. a unit that brings another unit later (Sarcophagus, Husk, Stitching Table) counts that unit at a discount. Giving Plague to enemies is valued 1 higher (AGREED, 10 Oct 2026): the cards that give Plague cost 1 more than before, with the same stats.
- Card text voice (AGREED, October 2026): a card refers to itself as I / me / my ("When I attack", "I return to your hand"); "it" always means another unit (e.g. Tourmarches: the ally that moved gains +1|+1). "In my lane" is assumed and not written; "in ANY lane" is written out where it applies (Ogre Thrower, Blood-Letter). Stat changes follow the wording in section 8 (plain verb for lasting, "this round", "have" for position bonuses). The tables in this document describe the abilities; the game's card texts are written in this voice.
- Drawbacks (AGREED): card text that hurts its own side (e.g. Misthophoroi deserting without carry-over) is a drawback, never an ability: it adds to the stat budget, so such a unit's strength + defence sits above the vanilla budget, never at or below it. Every ability is judged as benefit or drawback before it is costed.
- No faction gets a built-in weakness penalty (AGREED; the Exanimati included).
- So every unit's strength + defence = budget for its supply − size − keyword values − the value of its card text (+ the value of a drawback). The split between strength and defence is Claude's (open to change). Exception: Writhing Flesh (AGREED 1|1).
- Each faction has a baseline 1-cost unit showing its proclivity.
- 100 cards per faction, spells 20-50% of them (AGREED, 11 Oct 2026).
- Only mechanics are reused from other games, never names or wording.
- Design stays open-ended: future cards may create, multiply or steal cards, and values may grow very large.
- Decks may mix 2 factions later, so design cross-faction interactions.

==================================================================
10. KEYWORDS
==================================================================
Texts shown on hover in the game.

Movement, attack and position:
- Melee: Strikes enemies in its adjacent area; from your front row that area reaches across the base into the enemy front row (section 6).
- Ranged: Can attack any enemy in its lane, from any row.
- Pike: Also reaches the enemy front row from your second row (the cells facing it and diagonally next to them).
- Charge: When it is placed outside your front row, it charges straight forward as far as it can. If it reaches your front row, it strikes each enemy right ahead of it (in the enemy front row, in its columns) with its strength. Short panel: "When placed outside the front row, moves straight forward. If it reaches the front row, strikes the enemies right ahead."
- Lightfooted: Moves twice per round and pays only for the first move.
- Outrider: Its move can also take it to a free spot in another lane's camp, paid from the lane it leaves (a move, not an active ability).
- Immobile (Can't move): Can't move.
- Push: When its attack strikes a front-row enemy that survives, that enemy moves one row back, if the cells are free.
- Pull: The enemy moves straight forward, toward the base, as far as the cells are free.
- Defect: The enemy unit joins your side: it is placed in a free spot of your camp in the same lane (its Place effects trigger) and can't act that round. If no spot is free, nothing happens. Short panel: "Joins your side in a free spot of your camp in the same lane. Can't act that round."

Defence:
- Shield wall: Shield wall units standing directly beside each other in the same row give each other +1 defence (a unit with two such neighbours gets +2).
- Armoured: The first damage it takes each round is blocked, whatever its source (spells and your own strikes too).
- Link (AGREED): Link units of yours touching edges merge in place (they glow, no disappear-and-appear) into one unit of the combined shape at their current stats (bonuses included), adding strength, defence and supply and combining keywords; any connected shape; a merged unit never splits. Shield wall gives +1 defence only, so two Pavisarioi: 1|2 + 1|2 = 2|4. Built choices (Claude's, open to change): merging happens after every action and after round-end abilities; the older unit absorbs the newer; the merged unit counts as moved/attacked if either part had; temporary strength stays temporary; all merged cards go to the discard pile when it dies. Link may also suit the Exanimati (masses that unite).
- Bodyguard: When an ally adjacent to it would take damage, it takes it instead (not for self-strikes).
- Skirmisher: Has its bonus while no ally is adjacent.
- Incorporeal: Can't be struck, and units can't deal damage to it (attacks, Charge, Riposte, ability damage such as Akritai's or round-end damage, and strikes a spell makes a unit deal). Only spells can deal damage to it.

Damage:
- Strike (AGREED, October 2026): A hit with a unit's strength: its attacks and charges, and strikes with its strength from an ability or a spell. A melee unit strikes only what it could attack. Fixed amounts from abilities and spells are damage dealt, not strikes.
- Reckless: Dies right after it attacks (a charge strike counts).
- Overrun: When its strike kills, the leftover strength passes to the units right behind the target, split evenly (rounded down); each one it kills passes its leftover on the same way.
- Splash: Each unit its attacks strike passes half of that damage (rounded down) to the units right behind it, split evenly (rounded down), and so on backward.
- "Right behind" follows the struck unit's own cells: the units in the cells directly behind any of its cells (a 2-wide unit can pass to two units). A unit is struck once per attack; an amount that rounds down to 0 stops there.
- Pack: Has its bonus for each adjacent ally.
- Frenzy: While in your front row, whenever another ally in its lane dies, strikes the nearest enemy it could attack with its strength.
- Riposte (AGREED): When it survives an attack, it strikes the attacker back with its strength.
- Flurry (AGREED): Attacks twice per round and pays only for the first attack.
- Drain N (AGREED; keyword on the card since October 2026): When it drains a unit, it deals N damage to it and restores up to N of its own shields, even above its card's defence. The card's text says when and whom it drains.

Triggers:
- Place: Triggers every time the unit is placed on the grid: when you play it from your hand, when it defects to your side, when it is resurrected or returns, and when it appears as a token.
- Death: Triggers when this unit dies.
- Round end: A round ends when both players pass in a row. Round-end effects then fire in reading order, round starter first. A unit's strength bonuses add to the strikes its abilities deal to enemies.
- Supply: Adds this much extra supply to its lane at the start of each round.
- Sacrifice (AGREED; Horde and Exanimati): The unit can only be placed if its cells cover at least one of your units (and only yours); every unit it covers dies. A 2-cell unit can kill two units (e.g. a 1-cell unit and part of a 3-cell unit, if it covers both); a 3-cell unit covering only one 1-cell unit kills just that one.
(Round end, Place, Death and Supply need no keyword panels in the game: AGREED, 10 Oct 2026.)

Supply:
- Carry-over (keyword panel, October 2026): "Supply you keep between rounds. At round end your unspent lane supply is added to it, up to 5 in total; you can spend it in any lane." Shown on Misthophoroi, Gold Grubber, Forologia, Blood Money and Ossuary Tithe.

Spells:
- Everywhere (AGREED, October 2026): Paid from one lane, it works in all three lanes (Danse Macabre, Thinning of the Veil). No "Anywhere" keyword (rejected).

Death and the discard pile (Exanimati):
- Revenant (AGREED): When it dies, it is resurrected in the same cell (the resurrection rule: halved printed stats; it is placed, so its Place effects fire again) and keeps Revenant, so it can return again and again until its defence would halve to 0. It keeps its lasting changes (section 8).
- Plague (AGREED): Loses 1 shield at each round end; when it dies, adjacent units of both sides, in the camp where it died, catch Plague. "Catches Plague" = gains the Plague keyword.
- Afterlife N (AGREED): Death: places N Shades in free cells of your camp in its lane (front row first, reading order).
- Fading (AGREED): Dies at the end of the round in which it was placed.

States:
- Bloodied: Has less defence than its card shows. Defence gains that bring it back up to its card's value end it. Shown in a placed unit's hover panels.
- Blighted (AGREED as a few Exanimati cards; mostly for the Rotborn): Gives its lane 1 less supply each round (not below 0).

Removed (10 Oct 2026; don't reintroduce without asking): Rot, Wither, Freeze/Frozen, Fungal Remains (and the Fungal Corpse token, which moves to the future Rotborn as a concept).

Plain terms (hover text, no panel): back row (the row of your camp furthest from the base), row (the row or rows of its own camp the unit stands in), column (enemies in the same column face each other), directly in front (the cell next to it on the side of the base, same column), directly beside (the cell left or right of it, same row), adjacent, nearest enemy in its column (section 7).

Keyword panels exist only for the keywords above; other terms are plain text. No browser tooltips anywhere. Wherever a card shows its keyword panels beside it (hand, deck builder, a hovered unit, discard pile, deck list), its keywords have no underline and no hover of their own; only cards shown without panels (starting hand, Aurelius' pick, the spell slot) keep the dotted underline and the hover.
Card references (October 2026): every card named in a card's text or in its keywords' texts (found by name: Shade, Abomination, Doux, Gorvan, Stratiotes in Levy, the Crown, Sceptre and Blade on Aurelius, and any other) shows by default in the card's side panel, under its keyword panels, as a full card; its name in the text is bold, with no underline and no hover. Tokens have no flavour text, so a search never matches flavour.

13. UI

Layout:
- Fits one screen, fluid layout. No title bar. The play area (grids) should be as big as possible. Full screen: Start battle enters full screen; a toolbar button toggles it. In Chrome/Edge, Esc is kept by the game in full screen (hold Esc to leave).
- No sidebar. Top right: a Pass button, a Withdraw button (first click arms it and shows "Click again to withdraw" for 3 seconds; the second click ends the battle as a loss for the side that withdrew, with the end popup: Play again, Replay, Edit deck), a full-screen button and an info button that toggles a 50%-black panel (status, help, how to play, battle log, new game) covering the area right of the last lane. Refusal reasons show briefly as a message over the board.
- Deck: left side, a fat stack (thicker for each card), combined with the carry-over indicator (same symbol as other supply). Cards left shown on the deck, below the card-back symbol. A crown above the deck marks the side that acts first this round. Discard pile: right side, a mirrored stack, its top card shown larger than the decks' cards, with all its badges; hovering a discard pile shows its top card with its keywords explained, left of the pile, like a hovered unit. Spell slot between the two discard piles.
- Camp supply is shown under each camp, just outside the active-side panel.
- Active side: its three camps are wrapped by a light beige panel (lighter than the background), with the same small padding on every side and a gap to the bases.
- A clear banner announces each new round and who acts first; each side shows who started the round, who passed and who is to act. Both players show "In hand · N".
- Battle log lines are coloured by who did it (green you, red opponent); card names are highlighted, and hovering one shows that card left of the log panel.

Cards:
- All cards are the same size, except your hand (10% larger; stat badges keep their size). Card texts start large and shrink only as far as needed to fit.
- Stats along the top edge as large, easy-to-read numbers, no icons: strength top left (orange), supply top middle (bread symbol; red cost for spells), defence top right (blue). The unit's shape under the supply. The supply badge partly extrudes from the card's top edge. Strength and defence (AGREED, October 2026) are tabs that form the card's top-left and top-right corners: the tab's outer corner is the card's own rounded corner, the card's border runs around it, and its inner corner is rounded; overlapping cards in the hand don't hide them. A hand card carrying changes (section 8) shows its changed strength and defence tabs in green (higher) or red (lower).
- One symbol per card (AGREED, October 2026; no combined symbol-plus-mark icons). Every unit (tokens included) has a glyph of its own that no other unit uses, drawn the same on its card and on the board. Glyphs live in one library in the game file (ART); a glyph freed by a retired or changed unit stays in the library for reuse. Spells keep a single symbol from the same library and may share one with a unit.
- Spell cards have a different background colour from unit cards (no "Tactic" label). The strength and defence corner squares are small. Spell cost badges show "X" for Thousand Cuts and "=" for Bribe.
- Your hand sits at the bottom, showing each card down to its symbol (stats, shape and name visible without hovering). A hovered card rises only until its bottom sits just above the page edge, and its keywords show as black panels to its right (to its left near the right edge), followed by the cards its text names. The hand must never cover the lanes' supply symbols. Cards that can't be played now are greyed out (grey, not transparent); while a lane is hovered (also while one of your units is selected), the cards playable there jump up and the rest are greyed out.
- Enemy hand: at the top, a reversed version of yours, card backs only, pushed up to take little space.
- Keywords and named cards are explained in the side panels (section 10); on cards shown without side panels, keywords can be hovered.

Placed units:
- One top line: strength top left, supply top centre (bread with number), defence top right; on 1-cell units the three touch; numbers centred in their badges. The unit's body fills its cells, with the cells' rounded corners. Artwork (the unit's glyph) in the middle. Build-up dots stay. Only ranged units show a role icon (bottom centre). An unused Armoured unit's defence badge is shield-shaped.
- Stat colours: above its reference shows green, below shows red. Normally the reference is the printed card (so lost shields show the defence red); in previews and the eye's forecast it is the current value (a 5 previewed as 6 shows a green 6); a unit previewed as placed compares with its card, a moved one with its current values.
- The strength badge turns solid grey (grey badge, light grey number) when the unit can't attack again this round; the border turns grey when it can't move again; the whole unit dims when its lane can't pay for any action. Immobile units have a dashed inner border.
- Hovering a unit shows its full card with all its keywords explained in black boxes (and the cards its text names), placed beside the unit's lane, never over it (right if it fits, else left, keywords on the outer side). Not shown while aiming an action.
- Base block coloured by who holds it now, in muted tones (green: you / bottom side, red: opponent; plain if nobody). Who held it at round start is shown by a single frame around the block, set apart by a small gap, in the same green/red. Untaken dots are the base's plain grey-beige; a taken dot is filled green (you / bottom side) or red (opponent) with a light beige ring (AGREED, October 2026; was the army's colour).

Interaction and previews:
- Drag and drop, or click-then-click, to place and act. No buttons for moving. Right-click cancels a selection.
- Dragging a hand card (October 2026): the card itself is dragged from where it was grabbed (grabbing cursor; its slot in the hand stays empty). Over a camp a unit card disappears at once and the unit preview takes its place; a spell card shrinks smoothly beside the pointer over the board. Dropped off the board, the card glides back into the hand and is deselected.
- Placing (October 2026): one placement test serves deploys, moves, free moves and defector or resurrection spots. A unit that doesn't fit in the chosen orientation turns to one that fits by itself; only the free cells that can take it (in any orientation) get green dashed borders. A dashed white outline stays under the pointer while the preview shows the unit where it ends up (after a Charge); a unit that would die on the way is drawn where it dies, with the X.
- Clicking (or dragging) one of your units that can't move, attack or throw now doesn't select it; a message over the board says so.
- Hovering or selecting a card darkens the camps where a unit card can't be placed, or the lanes where a spell can't be cast; a follow-up choice darkens the lanes it can't act in (October 2026; replaces the blue and red lane frames). Hovering a lane lifts and outlines the cards that can be placed there and greys out the rest.
- Markers (AGREED, October 2026; one layer, the same for every card and action): they are drawn above the units, so a dimmed unit never dims them, each with a thin dark halo so they read on any colour; nothing pulses. Orange outline: a legal target (attack, spell, pick, throw), or a unit the hovered action or ability would strike, buff or pick. The hovered legal target gets a thicker outline with a light tint (for orange targets a lighter orange); this includes the unit a throw would land on. Red outline: a unit that would die (with an X), and attack targets the selected unit would kill. White outline: the selected unit, and a unit already picked in a two-step choice (Synaspismos' and Monomachia's first unit, a defector, a Place effect's picked target, the ally picked to be thrown). Areas: green dashed cells. Preview ghosts: dashed white border. No other borders or glows mark units.
- Reach, the same for every card: an ability's area (row, column, cells beside or in front, a whole camp, in either camp) gets green dashed cells, and every unit it would strike, buff or pick gets the orange marker (including units a follow-up pick could choose, and the ally in front of Menavlatoi). Damage aimed at the nearest enemy in the unit's column, or at the enemies facing it, shows that column of the enemy camp; strikes with the unit's strength (Manganon, Frenzy) show every cell in its reach. Shown when hovering a unit (yours or the enemy's), in placement and move previews (at the new spot, including Place effects), when hovering a spell's target, and during a follow-up choice.
- Column spells (Greek Fire, Contagion): hovering an enemy camp cell highlights that column (red dashed; grey if it can't be cast there). Lane-picking spells highlight the lane and the affected units on hover. Danse Macabre's hover shows the cells where the fallen would rise.
- Ogre Thrower (built): with it selected, it attacks as usual, and the allies it can throw get the orange marker; clicking one switches to throwing (that ally gets the white marker): the free cells it can land on show green in both camps of every lane, the units it can land on get the orange marker, and hovering a target previews the result: the thrown unit drawn where it lands (dashed white), or, landing on a unit, the damage and an X on the thrown unit. Right-click cancels.
- Previews of spells, attacks and kills show the consequences before confirming: units that die get an X and the red marker (and so does the supply they would no longer give), changed stats show their new values, supply cost and gains show in the supply indicators.
- Every preview is the hovered action played on a copy of the game and compared with the live game (never hand-coded per card). It also shows gained states (Plague, Blighted, Incorporeal marks), units that would appear (Shades from Afterlife, Last Rites, Hollow Bishop, Night of Shades, Corpse Candle; Abominations, Doux, returning Revenants, units rising from Danse Macabre, Gorvan…) drawn where they would stand with a dashed white border, and spreading or eating units (Writhing Flesh, Hungering Mass, the Stitching Table) in their grown shape.
- Placement and move previews draw the unit itself where it would end up (after a Charge, at the end of it), with a dashed white border and its resulting stats coloured; an invalid spot shows red, only on its free cells (never over other units); a selected unit with no move left shows no spots.
- Weeping Nun's reach covers its adjacent area.
- Previews of an action that waits for a choice show the part that doesn't depend on the choice (Cinder Imp: its damage to the enemy). Previews show the cards an action would draw or bring back as card backs at the end of that hand.
- Choosing a target for a Place effect: the hovered legal target gets the hovered marker; when picked, it gets the white marker (soft sound) before the effect plays.
- Supply in previews (AGREED, October 2026): the supply and carry-over the hovered action would take pulse in the camp supply and carry-over indicators, and what it would add shows as green bread, both taken from the action played on a copy of the game, net of its cost (Gold Grubber's attack paid from carry-over shows no change, Annona shows +2 net, Forologia and Ossuary Tithe show their carry-over gain); a placement also shows the supply the unit will add as extra green bread. Camp supply also shows, as extra empty bread, the supply the lane will give next round beyond this round's. The carry-over indicator always shows how much carry-over each side will have next round.
- Eye button between the two decks, level with the bases: while hovered, the whole UI shows the end of the round as if both sides passed now (round-end abilities applied, deaths and lost supply with X, stat changes, base ownership, next round's carry-over, the crown on who acts first next round). If the game would end, a banner over the board names the winner and the reason.
- Follow-up choices: a prompt bar at the very top says what to pick, with a Done button where the choice may be finished early. The Pass button then reads "Done" (or "Choose…" when required); Space finishes a choice that may be finished. Esc or right-click: a choice that may be finished early is finished; a required choice takes back the whole action (card back to the hand, supply refunded). The prompt bar says which.
- Thousand Cuts: hovering a unit previews spending all available supply on it; clicking casts it.
- Starting-hand screen: redrawn cards are greyed out; once 3 are redrawn, the rest show "Kept". The battle log states how many cards each side redrew.
- End popup: Play again, Replay, Edit deck (opens the deck you played in the deck builder; Save returns to the start page).
- Replay: the end popup has Replay; a panel offers a draggable slider over all the battle's moves (dragging pauses playback; releasing jumps to that move, forward or back, without animations), Play/Pause, Next, Restart, Exit, and lists the opponent's hand; same animations; units can be hovered.

14. ANIMATIONS AND SOUND
==================================================================
- Attack: attacker gets a red frame; after 0.5 s the target gets a red frame; the attacker's strength badge glows, then red markers fly to the target's defence (as many as the attacker's strength); the markers fly back.
- Damage starts from its real source (October 2026): a unit's strength badge for its strikes and ability damage, a dead unit's cell for its Death effects, the spell card only for spell damage.
- Hits of one step land together (October 2026): all the targets of an effect that happens at once are hit in the same animation.
- Deaths (October 2026): a unit that dies stays on the board, dimmed, and a tombstone drops onto it. While its death resolves, its cell gets a white border; every death whose chain is still running keeps its white border, and only the one being resolved right now has a lit (lighter) tombstone. When its chain is done, the dead unit flies to the discard pile. Stat gains from each death show the moment it resolves.
- Charge (October 2026): the charger sets off fast (gains such as Tourmarches' show as it sets off), rushes into the enemies ahead, knocks them back a little, the lane shakes front to back, and everyone settles into their cells.
- Throw (October 2026): the thrown unit flies in an arc to its cell; landing on a unit it hits it (no markers) and dies there.
- Mass Grave (October 2026): all the fliers glide together through the enemy camp without stopping; each vanishes where it strikes, or flies off the board; an enemy turns into a grave the moment its killing flier passes over it, although the strikes all count at the end of the spell.
- Drain: drops of life stream from the target into the drainer.
- Units killed outright (by a spell, a sacrifice or Reckless) get their tombstone the same way; damage blocked by armour shows an "Armour" label.
- Movement (especially the enemy's): green border on the unit, green highlighted destination, then the unit slides there.
- Placement: yellow border for 1 second plus a big yellow puff (also for a thrown unit landing on a free cell, and for units rising from Danse Macabre).
- Restored shields: the unit glows green and "+n" rises from its defence, with a soft chime.
- Stat gains: whenever a unit's strength or full defence goes up (auras, buffs, Link merges, growth), green rings puff once from that stat badge.
- Spells: the played card flies face up (the opponent's flips over) to the spell slot and stays there about 1 second for the opponent's spells (0.6 s for your own) before its effects play; while it acts, the lane it works on is highlighted in orange (Greek Fire and Contagion: the column, red dashed) and a picked unit gets the orange marker; then it flies to the discard pile. Spells cast on a lane only to pay (Levy, Crown of Aurelius, Ossuary Tithe, Bone Harvest, Requiem, Danse Macabre, Thinning of the Veil) highlight no lane.
- Card draw: a card flies from the deck into the hand, with a sound.
- Cards entering a hand from anywhere else animate too (AGREED, October 2026): a created card (Levy's Stratiotes) appears at the spell slot and flies into the hand; a card returning from the discard pile (Undying Reaver, Clerk of the Dead Ledger, Requiem) flies from the pile. The opponent's show their back. Draws and arrivals fill the hand's new slots in the order they happen (a card drawn by a death at round end takes the first new slot, the round's draws the next ones), so the hand never shows a gap.
- Unspent supply flies into the carry-over indicator at round end.
- Timing: the computer waits 1 second between actions, counted from the end of the previous action's animation, and 1 second more when it passes. When you have no actions left, your turn passes automatically after 2 seconds; a computer with no actions passes after 1 second.
- Sound: a clock tick on each change of turn, a gong for each new round, card drawing, unit placement, each strike or damage, unit death, unit movement (a group marching, footsteps matching the distance moved) and base capture. Currently synthesized in the page; to be replaced by free sound files later.

In the game (three-bases-game.html):
- The Benchmark (default): the AI lab's ifelse AI with the "ifelse AI gen 18" values (the earlier Benchmark is retired from the game). It sees the opponent's hand and searches the real position (alpha-beta, scored as the round would end). Follow-up choices are plies of the same player in its look-ahead (so after a Place pick it sees one ply less far). Thousand Cuts always spends all available supply, so it is one option per target. A unit's worth adds a rough, hand-set bonus for its ability (e.g. Ranged and unused Armoured 8, Pike 4, row auras 6 per point given; drawbacks such as Reckless or self-strikes subtract a few points); the Exanimati cards have no such values yet. Its threat estimate ignores a single strike on an unused Armoured unit and any strike on an Incorporeal unit. It considers Onslaught only in lanes where it would move at least one unit. Redraw: cards costing 4 or more, the most expensive first, at most 3 (Bribe and Thousand Cuts count as cost 0).
- Reworked (October 2026) on top of the gen 18 values: it never casts a spell that would change nothing (no board, hand, deck or supply change beyond its own card and cost; units are compared by card, not by id, so Last Rites on a Shade, which only swaps a Shade for a Shade, counts as nothing), never casts Reform Ranks or makes a free move (Reform Ranks, Mandator) unless one of the moves makes the round better than making none (October 2026; it used to move a unit there and back again), never places Blood-Letter when its Place pick would have no target, and doesn't consider Monomachia when neither unit has strength; at its root choice, an action that kills enemy units counts their worth extra (killPref) and passing right after the opponent passed counts 40 less (passPen 40); "two bases" counts only bases held at round start and now (the win rule; in its look-ahead the next round starts with the current bases). An evaluation rebuilt from scratch on the round-end forecast was tried and lost (3 of 12), so the tuned values stay.
- Kills over carry-over (October 2026): it valued each point of carry-over at about 61 (an enemy unit is worth about 20–40), so it passed to save supply instead of killing. Now carry 30 and killPref 1 (was 61 and 0.5): against the previous version it won 15 of 24 games (8 lost, 1 stopped) on mixed premade-deck pairings.
- Tournament check (10 Oct 2026): every premade deck against every premade deck, both seats (162 games per run), the reworked Benchmark against the version before the Reform Ranks and free-move fix. Played at search depth 1 for speed (2 cores; the real depth 3 takes about 1 s per move, a full run would take hours). Result over three seed sets: 250 wins to 228, i.e. even. An extra term valuing a unit's abilities by the design budget was tried and showed no gain, so it is not in the game; a weight-by-weight search (each value halved or doubled) found nothing that held up on fresh seeds. A real improvement needs tuning at the true depth over many hours, or new evaluation terms for the new rules (tombstones, deaths in chains, abilities).
- Known: it has no hand-set values for the new cards; retuning on the current cards belongs to the AI lab.
- The Random player: picks a kind of move at random (deploy, move, attack, spell or pass), then a move of that kind; redraws 0-3 random cards.
- Second player on the same screen.

16. AI LAB (three-bases-ai-lab.html)
==================================================================
A separate page for training learning AIs, pitting AIs against each other, and watching games. It uses the game's rules and the same network code, but still the earlier card list (to be moved to the current cards; then the learning AIs are retrained). Everything runs in the browser; progress is saved in the browser (networks in IndexedDB). Only one lab tab can run at a time. Never remove the lab or anything related to it.

Players in the lab:
- The Benchmark (lab version, the earlier one): a look-ahead AI that sees the opponent's actual hand. For a move it's considering, it examines every legal action (every placement counts on its own; none are merged as "similar"), imagines the opponent's best replies, then its own best answers to those, and judges the resulting position as if both sides passed from there (the same resolution as the eye). It refuses actions that spend supply for no effect, and plays to avoid losing to the out-of-supply and inactivity rules. Redraws cards costing 4 or more, the most expensive first, at most 3.
- The Strategist: the same look-ahead approach, but it never looks at the opponent's hand (it assumes they could hold any card of their faction not yet seen; it looks ahead over random possible opponent hands); each turn it picks two "goal" lanes to fight for and follows a few rules of thumb (don't keep deploying into a lane already safely won; don't deploy outside its goal lanes unless it blocks an enemy dot). Values can no longer be edited by hand (the variant form was removed); variants saved earlier stay as players and can be deleted. Redraws like the Benchmark.
- The ifelse AI: sees both hands and searches the real position (alpha-beta, scored as the round would end), with no rules of thumb; every judgement is a weighted term and every value is tunable (see the ifelse AI tab). Exported with "Export ifelse AI" as a named file for the game. Redraws like the Benchmark.
- The Random player.
- Learning AIs (section below), playing alone or inside the Benchmark's search.
- Dropped: a "Planner" and a "Commander" were tried; both played worse than the Benchmark.

Learning AIs (all share the same network design; on loading, the lab deletes the records and networks of AIs it no longer has, and any other lab storage key or stored network it no longer uses, leaving the games' keys alone):
- Tabula rasa: starts knowing nothing and learns only from its own training games.
- Added trainers: "+ New trainer" under the AI boards asks for a name (suggested "Trainer 1", "Trainer 2", …; it must differ from every other learning AI's name) and adds a plain trainer that learns like Tabula rasa and starts from nothing. "Load network file" loads any exported network into it to train it further. Added trainers are training opponents, part of "a mix", and tournament players like the others; "Remove" (added trainers only) deletes the trainer with its network and record, and anything training against it trains against itself from then on.
- All learning networks of the same shape start from the same fixed starting weights on every restart (fair comparison).
- Ways to play: alone (scores the board after each of its moves and plays the best; guesses the opponent's hand), with the reply check (also scores the opponent's replies to its 8 best moves), or inside the Benchmark's search (the Benchmark's look-ahead with the network's scores; sees the hand).
- Exports: "Export training" (the network with its training record) and "Export network" / "Export network only" (the network alone, under a name chosen when exporting, suggested as the AI and its latest test win rate against the Benchmark, e.g. "Tabula rasa 74%.json").

The network (format 5):
- It judges a position as seen by the player about to act and outputs one number: that player's chance of winning. To choose a move, the program scores the board after each legal move.
- Inputs: every card has a learned dictionary row (16 numbers) plus its printed values. Each of the 54 board cells passes through one shared cell layer that also knows the cell's side, row and column; a unit is described once at its anchor cell (current strength, defence, damage, build-ups, temporary strength, moved, attacked, orientation, its dictionary row and printed values), and the other cells it covers are marked occupied with a pointer to the anchor cell. The hand is 10 slots (card-list order). Own deck, the opponent's unseen cards (hand and deck), the own dealt starting hand and the own redrawn cards are counted per card. Per lane: dots, base owner, supply left, round-start supply, lane supply (both sides). Per side: carry-over (its own input, never merged into lane supply), hand size, deck size, discard size. Game: round, who started, whose turn, who passed, rounds without action, actions this round, factions, and the opponent's redraw count once the battle has begun. Discard piles are given by size only. Defence and damage are separate inputs.
- How cells are combined now: the 54 cell summaries are laid side by side into the first main layer, which has its own weights per cell, so relations between cells (behind, beside) are learned separately for every pair of cells, with no built-in idea of adjacency.
- Scale: values with no firm limit use sqrt(x)/10 (1 -> 0.10, 2 -> 0.14, 5 -> 0.22, 100 -> 1.00, larger keeps growing; never below 0); values with a firm rule limit are divided by it (carry-over 5, hand 10, rounds without action 3, redraws 3). Stored as decimals, no caps.
- Size: main layers of 128 and 64 nodes. If cards or printed values are added later, saved networks keep what they learned; new parts start fresh.
- Redraw: a learning AI scores its opening; for each dealt card not yet redrawn it weighs every possible replacement by its chance (copies in deck / deck size) and averages the network's scores; it redraws the card with the best expected improvement, if any, and repeats, at most 3 times.

Training:
- Lessons: every position of a training game (both sides) with the game's result for the side to act: 1 won, 0.5 drawn, 0 lost. Games the lab stops as too long (round 40 or 500 actions) are not learned from. Each learning step uses up to 64 lessons. Each position is learned from a set number of times (default 8). Lessons are also learned with lanes 1 and 3 swapped (mirroring checkbox, on by default).
- "Search in training" (per AI, off by default; it replaced "own later judgement"): in its games against itself or its training opponent (not library games) the AI chooses each of its moves with a look-ahead: the boards after all its legal moves are scored by its network, its 4 best are kept, for each of them every opponent reply (with one randomly guessed opponent hand) is scored, each move counts as its worst reply, and the move with the best of those is played (random moves still happen at the "random moves" rate). That value (its estimated chance to win) is stored with the position, and the position's target becomes (1 − share) × the real result + share × that value; share is a per-AI setting, 0.5 by default. Positions not chosen this way (the opponent's moves, random moves) keep the real result. The size check's networks learn the same targets. One lesson per position; games are slower. Changes apply from the next game.
- Step size (Adam, starts at 0.0005, never below a floor of 0.001/32 ≈ 0.00003): judged on the win rate in the scheduled tests against the test opponent (playing alone), with two weighted moving averages as in MACD: 9 tests (fast) and 18 tests (slow), each new test weighing 2/(N+1). Judging starts once 18 tests count. When the fast average has not been more than 1 point above the slow one for 3 tests in a row, the step size halves; then 9 tests pass before judging again (also after a training-opponent change, a Reset, or the end of a boost or cycle). Every scheduled test counts; "Test now" tests do not count. Each size-check network is judged on its own tests. Controls next to the step size: Reset (back to 0.0005); Boost (to 0.00025, then halved at each test until the floor, then the usual rule); Cycle, a toggle showing the current step and its direction (↑/↓): halves at each test down to the floor, then doubles at each test up to 0.00025, a full cycle every 6 tests, overriding the usual rule until switched off (the step then stays where it is).
- Training opponent (per AI, changeable live): itself (default), the Benchmark, the Strategist, the ifelse AI (with its current tuned values), another learning AI (playing alone), or a mix (each game at random, one of the mix's members; the ifelse AI can be a member). The opponent list is a dropdown; hovering (or focusing) "a mix" opens a side panel listing who can be in the mix (itself, the Benchmark, the Strategist, each other learning AI) as checkboxes, with an "All" button; at least one stays ticked, and changing the mix also chooses "a mix" as the opponent. The dropdown shows the mix as "a mix (all)" or "a mix (N of M)". With everyone ticked, AIs added later join the mix too; otherwise only the ticked members are used. A mix change counts as an opponent change for the step-size rule. "Export for analysis" lists the mix's members.
- Size check (per AI, switched on or off at any time; a running training pauses and resumes around the change): "More nodes" (256, 128) and "Extra layer" (128, 64, 32) learn the same lessons alongside the AI's own network, each with its own step size, starting at the AI's step size at that moment (with its boost or cycle, if on). Both start as copies of the AI's network as it is when switched on. More nodes keeps every weight; its new nodes get the usual random incoming weights and zero outgoing weights and biases, so it starts playing exactly like the original. Extra layer keeps everything up to the 64-node layer; its new 32-node layer and its output start fresh (an exact copy is impossible there). Their tests count from the switch-on. Off removes both networks and their results; switching on again makes fresh copies.
- Cores: "Games at once" is the total (a change applies at once, also while training); it is shared by the AIs training at once, and each AI moves cores between learners and games as needed; during a test, the learners' waiting lessons are gathered into as few learners as can hold them (usually one) and the other cores play test games. New training games are started only while the waiting lessons, plus what the games already being played will bring (by the recent average per game), stay below the queue limits.
- Tests: every 1000 training games (setting) each AI plays the test opponent playing alone, 100 games by default (setting); "Test now" plays 100. The test opponent is one setting for all AIs, at the top of the Training tab ("Test against …"): the Benchmark (default), the Strategist, or a network loaded from a file (from "Export network" or "Export training"), kept frozen and stored in the browser with the lab's networks. It is fixed for a test when that test starts; each test records whom it was against, and the chart marks with a dashed line, labelled "tests vs …", where the opponent changed. A change pauses every AI's step-size judging for 9 tests, as after a training-opponent change.
- Teacher-game library: the Benchmark v Strategist games played earlier for teaching are kept in the browser's database as the board after the starting-hand redraws plus the list of moves (after the redraws the rules involve no chance, so replaying the moves gives exactly the same game). No new games are added. The Progress row "Library games learned from" shows how many an AI has learned from. "Learn from library (N)": the AI takes the library games it has not learned from yet, in whole test intervals (N), learning from them as teacher games (positions with results), with tests continuing at the usual interval; then it returns to its usual training. The chart shades that stretch as "the teacher-game library", and the header shows "learning from the teacher-game library".
- Speed: the network's input is encoded straight into its input memory, the opponent's unseen cards are counted from precomputed deck counts, and auras and "beside" checks avoid repeated work; moves, encodings and results are identical (verified on seeded games), network self-play about 1.3x faster. The Benchmark's look-ahead, the Strategist's look-ahead (inside each guessed hand; gain about 5-10%) and a learning AI inside the Benchmark's search use alpha-beta pruning; the Benchmark is about twice as fast, with identical moves.

Screens:

- Chart: test wins (solid, with dots), each with its 9-test average (dotted) and 18-test average (wide, faint); prediction scores (dashed); the main network's step size as a red stepped line on a right-hand axis (0 to 0.001; ticks only at the values the step can take, 0.001 halved down to the floor, labelled where they do not crowd), drawn from when it was first recorded, with a hover line and a box listing every value. Clicking a legend label shows or hides that line (a wins line together with its two averages), remembered per AI. Periods of training against different opponents are shaded in different background colours.
- Tournament tab: pick players (the coded players, the ifelse AI, and each learning AI playing alone or inside the Benchmark search) and run round-robin games; results can be copied as text.
- ifelse AI tab: tunes all the ifelse AI's values at once with CMA-ES against a mix of opponents ticked from: Benchmark, Strategist and each learning AI in the lab (playing as in its tests: no look-ahead); at least one ticked; games are shared equally among them, 12 in a row each so that every block covers all matchups and both seats. Every value lives on one common scale (0 = its starting value): weights move by a factor e^x (a weight starting at 0 by 5·x), shares on the logit scale (×2), whole numbers by x times their range (rounded, within their limits), switches flip when x crosses ±0.25. Each generation, the candidates (10 to begin with) are sampled around the centre with the learned step size and covariance; each plays "Games per candidate" games (default 300) on the same schedule; win rate with draws counting half; the centre moves toward the better half (standard CMA-ES weights and updates; step size capped at 1). When the generation average hasn't improved by more than 1 point for 20 generations, or the step size falls below 0.02, it restarts around the current centre with twice the candidates (up to 40), marked on the chart. Changing the mix restarts only the generation under way (marked). Start, Pause/Resume, Start over (back to the starting values), "Export ifelse AI", its own "Games at once". One thing runs at a time: starting the tuning stops training and pauses a tournament, and starting either pauses the tuning. Chart: per generation, the candidates' average win rate (line) and the best candidate's (dot). Table: every value, starting value vs the centre (changes highlighted). The ifelse AI plays with the centre's values as a tournament player; "Export for analysis" includes its state.
- ifelse AI values (starting values = the earlier Benchmark's; the Strategist's for goal lanes, which start off): moves looked ahead (3-5, starts 3) and moves examined at each of the five levels (8, 6, 4, 4, 4); skip moves that change nothing (switch, on); worth of a base 220, a dot 45; with one base, per own dot in the other lanes 30; two bases +300, opponent's two bases -450; lane income 12 per supply, counted up to 6, share 0.3 in a won lane; unspent supply 3; carry-over 10; cards in hand 4 (own minus opponent's); supply cost of cards in hand 0; unit worth per supply 10, strength 2, defence 3, (supply + supply bonus) 5, front-row bonus 6; won lane: 2 front units in full, others 0.1; contested lane: each further unit 0.15 less, at least 0.3; threat multiplier 1, kill share 0.6, damage 2, side-to-move weights 1 / 0.3 (opponent to move) and 0.9 / 0.3 (own move); threat switches (all on): each attacker used once with supply shared and several attackers able to finish one target together, Bodyguards protect neighbours, units that attacked this round threaten nothing; out of supply with 0/1/2 units 1000/300/100; a draw -500; goal lanes (switch, off) with third-lane share 0.7, stick 1.5 and lane-need weights 2, 1.5, 0.15, 0.1, 0.2. Cards in the opponent's hand are covered by the search itself, which sees them.
- "Export for analysis" downloads everything measured (settings, every AI's record and tests, size checks, tournament) as one file to examine.
