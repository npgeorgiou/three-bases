==================================================================
15. COMPUTER OPPONENTS
==================================================================
In the game (three-bases-game.html):
- The Benchmark (default): the AI lab's ifelse AI with the "ifelse AI gen 18" values. It sees the opponent's hand and searches the real position (alpha-beta, scored as the round would end). Its threat estimate counts the attacks the opponent's units could make. Redraw: cards costing 4 or more, the most expensive first, at most 3.
- Reworked (October 2026) on top of the gen 18 values: at its root choice, an action that kills enemy units counts their worth extra (killPref) and passing right after the opponent passed counts 40 less (passPen 40); it skips a move or attack that spends supply and changes nothing in how the round ends (skipPointless); "two bases" counts only bases held at round start and now (the win rule; in its look-ahead the next round starts with the current bases). An evaluation rebuilt from scratch on the round-end forecast was tried and lost (3 of 12), so the tuned values stay.
- Kills over carry-over (October 2026): it valued each point of carry-over at about 61 (an enemy unit is worth about 20–40), so it passed to save supply instead of killing. Now carry 30 and killPref 1 (was 61 and 0.5): against the previous version it won 15 of 24 games (8 lost, 1 stopped) on mixed premade-deck pairings.
- Keyword values: a unit's worth can add, per keyword, its value x the card's number for it x how much it works now. js/ai/keyword-worth.js has one line per keyword with that rule, following the keyword's text: Ranged and Immobile count 1. A new keyword gets its line when it is made; tools/ifelse_speed.js lists keywords on unit cards with no line. The lab makes one tunable value per line whose keyword is in the cards (kw_<field>). BENCH_VALUES has none, so they add nothing.
- First moves by the round end (switch rootRound, 9 Nov 2026; off in the Benchmark, on in the lab's ifelse AI): the own moves examined at the first step are picked by their value after the round end is played out instead of the position right after the move, so a move that pays off only at round end is not dropped before the search looks at it. Speed (tools/ifelse_speed.js, 12 fixed positions, depth 3): the Benchmark (16 x 16 moves) 1.5 s per move; with 10 x 10, the round-end pick and keyword values 0.7 s. Asking the skip test only of the best-scored moves until enough are kept (9 Nov 2026) gives the same decisions and takes a quarter less time at depth 1.
- An ifelse AI from the AI lab (10 Nov 2026): in the setup dialog's "Computer player" list, "An ifelse AI from the AI lab (choose its file)" opens a file picker for a file made by the lab's "Export ifelse AI"; the list then shows "ifelse AI: <name>", with "Other file" beside it. The file carries the values and how far the AI looks, and the game plays it exactly as it was tuned. A file without its look-ahead is refused with a line of text. The choice and the file are remembered in the browser. Redraws like the Benchmark.
- The Random player: picks a kind of move at random (deploy, move, attack or pass), then a move of that kind; redraws 0-3 random cards.
- Second player on the same screen.
- Not in the game yet: the tunable ifelse AI and the learning AIs (lab only).
- Speed: the look-ahead uses alpha-beta pruning (skips lines that cannot change the choice) and fast board-geometry helpers; moves are identical to the unpruned search (verified on seeded games).
