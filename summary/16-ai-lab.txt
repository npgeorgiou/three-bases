==================================================================
16. AI LAB (three-bases-ai-lab.html)
==================================================================
A separate page for training learning AIs, pitting AIs against each other, and watching games. It is built from src/lab/ together with the game's own card, rules and computer code, so it always plays the game's current cards and rules (moved to the cards of 4 Nov 2026 on 4 Nov 2026). Everything runs in the browser; progress is saved in the browser (networks in IndexedDB). Only one lab tab can run at a time. Never remove the lab or anything related to it.
- Decks: the game's 14 premade decks. Training games take a random pair of them (any two, mirror decks included); test games and ifelse tuning go through the pairs in a fixed mixed order, each pair for both seats. Tournament matchups: "Different factions" and "Same faction". Results by faction list every faction. Saved networks and records made for the earlier cards are not used (each AI starts over), tournament results from the earlier cards are left out of the tables, and the teacher-game library is empty (its games were played with the earlier cards).

Players in the lab (the Strategist was removed, 4 Nov 2026):
- The Benchmark: the game's Benchmark (section 15), the same code and values. The earlier Benchmark's score and look-ahead are still used by the learning AIs' "Benchmark search" (its move, the opponent's best replies, its own best answers to those).
- The ifelse AI: sees both hands and searches the real position (alpha-beta, scored as the round would end), with no rules of thumb; every judgement is a weighted term and every value is tunable (see the ifelse AI tab). Exported with "Export ifelse AI" as a named file for the game. Redraws like the Benchmark.
- The Random player.
- Learning AIs (section below), playing alone or inside the Benchmark's search.
- Dropped: a "Planner" and a "Commander" were tried; both played worse than the Benchmark.

Learning AIs (all share the same network design; on loading, the lab deletes the records and networks of AIs it no longer has, and any other lab storage key or stored network it no longer uses, leaving the games' keys alone):
- Tabula rasa: starts knowing nothing and learns only from its own training games.
- Added trainers: "+ New trainer" under the AI boards asks for a name (suggested "Trainer 1", "Trainer 2", …; it must differ from every other learning AI's name) and adds a plain trainer that learns like Tabula rasa and starts from nothing. "Load network file" loads any exported network into it to train it further. Added trainers are training opponents, part of "a mix", and tournament players like the others; "Remove" (added trainers only) deletes the trainer with its network and record, and anything training against it trains against itself from then on.
- All learning networks of the same shape start from the same fixed starting weights on every restart (fair comparison).
- Ways to play: alone (scores the board after each of its moves and plays the best; guesses the opponent's hand), with the reply check (also scores the opponent's replies to its 8 best moves), or inside the Benchmark's search (the earlier Benchmark's look-ahead with the network's scores; sees the hand).
- Exports: "Export training" (the network with its training record) and "Export network" / "Export network only" (the network alone, under a name chosen when exporting, suggested as the AI and its latest test win rate against the Benchmark, e.g. "Tabula rasa 74%.json").

The network (format 3, version 8; networks of version 7 cannot be used and start over):
- It judges a position as seen by the player about to act and outputs one number: that player's chance of winning. To choose a move, the program scores the board after each legal move.
- Inputs: no card has weights of its own; a card is seen only by what it is and does (its printed values): its numbers and keywords (cost, size, strength, defence, Geared ...) and its effects in parts, read from the card data: when + what ("roundEnd:strike"), what + on whom ("strike>nearest"), with amounts; auras and spells the same way (640 values on the cards of 5 Nov 2026). A reworked card is a new mix of known parts; only a word no card had before is new to the network. Each of the 54 board cells passes through one shared cell layer that also knows the cell's side, row and column; a unit is described once at its anchor cell (current strength, defence, damage, build-ups, temporary strength, moved, attacked, orientation, its printed values, and what changed in play: each keyword as it is now minus as printed, Incorporeal and Armoured for this round, Reckless, Armoured used, moves and attacks made, deployed this round, charged, blighted), and the other cells it covers are marked occupied with a pointer to the anchor cell. The hand is 10 slots (card-list order), each the card's printed values. Four piles (own deck, the opponent's unseen cards in hand and deck, the own dealt starting hand, the own redrawn cards), each as its card count and the totals of its cards' printed values / 10. Per lane: dots, base owner, supply left, round-start supply, lane supply (both sides). Per side: carry-over, hand size, deck size, discard size. Game: round, who started, whose turn, who passed, rounds without action, actions this round, the opponent's redraw count once the battle has begun, and per side the deaths and spells of this round. No faction inputs. Speed: network alone about 9 ms per move (was 76 ms when every one of the 753 cards was counted on its own in each pile).
- How cells are combined now: the 54 cell summaries are laid side by side into the first main layer, which has its own weights per cell, so relations between cells (behind, beside) are learned separately for every pair of cells, with no built-in idea of adjacency.
- Scale: values with no firm limit use sqrt(x)/10 (1 -> 0.10, 2 -> 0.14, 5 -> 0.22, 100 -> 1.00, larger keeps growing; never below 0); values with a firm rule limit are divided by it (carry-over 5, hand 10, rounds without action 3, redraws 3). Stored as decimals, no caps.
- Size: main layers of 128 and 64 nodes. Saved networks keep what they learned when printed values or the rules' keyword list change: their weights are moved by the value's name (printed values and unit-state values alike), new values start at zero; changed or new cards need nothing (check: tools/net_byname_check.js).
- Redraw: a learning AI scores its opening; for each dealt card not yet redrawn it weighs every possible replacement by its chance (copies in deck / deck size) and averages the network's scores; it redraws the card with the best expected improvement, if any, and repeats, at most 3 times.

Training:
- Lessons: every position of a training game (both sides) with a target for the side to act, worked out from the last position back: target = 70% the next position's target + 30% the AI's own win chance at the next position (both as seen by the side to act); the last position's target is the game's result: 1 won, 0.5 drawn, 0 lost. Library games teach the result only. Games the lab stops as too long (round 40 or 500 actions) are not learned from. Each learning step uses up to 64 lessons. Each position is learned from a set number of times (default 8). Lessons are also learned with lanes 1 and 3 swapped (mirroring checkbox, on by default).
- "Search in training" (per AI, off by default; it replaced "own later judgement"): in its games against itself or its training opponent (not library games) the AI chooses each of its moves with a look-ahead: the boards after all its legal moves are scored by its network, its 4 best are kept, for each of them every opponent reply (with one randomly guessed opponent hand) is scored, each move counts as its worst reply, and the move with the best of those is played (random moves still happen at the "random moves" rate). That value (its estimated chance to win) is stored with the position, and the position's target becomes (1 − share) × the real result + share × that value; share is a per-AI setting, 0.5 by default. Positions not chosen this way (the opponent's moves, random moves) keep the real result. The size check's networks learn the same targets. One lesson per position; games are slower. Changes apply from the next game.
- Step size (Adam, starts at 0.0005, never below a floor of 0.001/32 ≈ 0.00003): judged on the win rate in the scheduled tests against the test opponent (playing alone), with two weighted moving averages as in MACD: 9 tests (fast) and 18 tests (slow), each new test weighing 2/(N+1). Judging starts once 18 tests count. When the fast average has not been more than 1 point above the slow one for 3 tests in a row, the step size halves; then 9 tests pass before judging again (also after a training-opponent change, a Reset, or the end of a boost or cycle). Every scheduled test counts; "Test now" tests do not count. Each size-check network is judged on its own tests. Controls next to the step size: Reset (back to 0.0005); Boost (to 0.00025, then halved at each test until the floor, then the usual rule); Cycle, a toggle showing the current step and its direction (↑/↓): halves at each test down to the floor, then doubles at each test up to 0.00025, a full cycle every 6 tests, overriding the usual rule until switched off (the step then stays where it is).
- Training opponent (per AI, changeable live): itself (default), the Benchmark, the ifelse AI (with its current tuned values), another learning AI (playing alone), or a mix (each game at random, one of the mix's members). The opponent list is a dropdown; hovering (or focusing) "a mix" opens a side panel listing who can be in the mix (itself, the Benchmark, the ifelse AI, each other learning AI) as checkboxes, with an "All" button; at least one stays ticked, and changing the mix also chooses "a mix" as the opponent. The dropdown shows the mix as "a mix (all)" or "a mix (N of M)". With everyone ticked, AIs added later join the mix too; otherwise only the ticked members are used. A mix change counts as an opponent change for the step-size rule. "Export for analysis" lists the mix's members.
- Size check (per AI, switched on or off at any time; a running training pauses and resumes around the change): "More nodes" (256, 128) and "Extra layer" (128, 64, 32) learn the same lessons alongside the AI's own network, each with its own step size, starting at the AI's step size at that moment (with its boost or cycle, if on). Both start as copies of the AI's network as it is when switched on. More nodes keeps every weight; its new nodes get the usual random incoming weights and zero outgoing weights and biases, so it starts playing exactly like the original. Extra layer keeps everything up to the 64-node layer; its new 32-node layer and its output start fresh (an exact copy is impossible there). Their tests count from the switch-on. Off removes both networks and their results; switching on again makes fresh copies.
- Lessons check (per AI, the box below Size check; it uses the same two extra networks, so turning one on turns the other off): "Next move 15%" and "Next move 45%" start as exact copies of the AI's network when switched on and learn from the same games and steps. In the AI's own training games each copy's target is worked out from the last position back: target = (1 − share) × the next position's target + share × that copy's own win chance at the next position (both as seen by the side to act; the last position keeps the real result), with share 0.15 or 0.45; with "Search in training" on, that target then mixes with the search's value as usual. The AI's own network keeps its 30%. Library games teach all three the real result. Tests, step sizes and switching off work as for the size check.
- Cores: "Games at once" is the total (a change applies at once, also while training); it is shared by the AIs training at once, and each AI moves cores between learners and games as needed; during a test, the learners' waiting lessons are gathered into as few learners as can hold them (usually one) and the other cores play test games. New training games are started only while the waiting lessons, plus what the games already being played will bring (by the recent average per game), stay below the queue limits.
- "Benchmark depth in training and tests" (Training tab; 1, 2 or 3, starts 1): how far the Benchmark looks ahead as training opponent and as test opponent; tests record it ("the Benchmark (depth 1)"), so a change is marked on the chart like a new opponent. Measured on 40 positions (4 Nov 2026): depth 1 about 0.03 s per move, depth 2 about 0.24 s, depth 3 about 1.7 s; depth 1 and depth 2 pick the same move as depth 3 in 65% and 68% of positions, and when they differ their move is worse by about a dot (64-72 points) by depth 3's own scoring. Tournaments and watching use the full depth; the check match uses the tuning depth.
- Test games (scheduled and "Test now") are dealt the same way every time (each pair of games one deal), so tests at different times compare the same games.
- Tests: every 1000 training games (setting) each AI plays the test opponent playing alone, 100 games by default (setting); "Test now" plays 100. The test opponent is one setting for all AIs, at the top of the Training tab ("Test against …"): the Benchmark (default) or a network loaded from a file (from "Export network" or "Export training"), kept frozen and stored in the browser with the lab's networks. It is fixed for a test when that test starts; each test records whom it was against, and the chart marks with a dashed line, labelled "tests vs …", where the opponent changed. A change pauses every AI's step-size judging for 9 tests, as after a training-opponent change.
- Teacher-game library: the games played earlier between coded AIs for teaching are kept in the browser's database as the board after the starting-hand redraws plus the list of moves (after the redraws the rules involve no chance, so replaying the moves gives exactly the same game). No new games are added. The Progress row "Library games learned from" shows how many an AI has learned from. "Learn from library (N)": the AI takes the library games it has not learned from yet, in whole test intervals (N), learning from them as teacher games (positions with results), with tests continuing at the usual interval; then it returns to its usual training. The chart shades that stretch as "the teacher-game library", and the header shows "learning from the teacher-game library".
- Speed: the network's input is encoded straight into its input memory, the opponent's unseen cards are counted from precomputed deck counts, and auras and "beside" checks avoid repeated work; moves, encodings and results are identical (verified on seeded games), network self-play about 1.3x faster. The Benchmark's look-ahead, a learning AI inside the Benchmark's search use alpha-beta pruning; the Benchmark is about twice as fast, with identical moves.

Screens:
- Each AI has a board: settings on the left; on the right, the action buttons in one horizontal row (Train, Test now, Watch a game, Export training, Export network, Load network file, Learn from library, Start over; Remove on added trainers) above progress and statistics (two fixed columns with the same rows in the same places for every AI: games and lessons on the left, results and learning on the right; rows that do not apply show a dash; compact values: games ended as "won | pass | draw | stop", predicts the winner as "new positions · lessons", by game stage as "%: E x (y) | M … | L …" with the Benchmark's figure in grey; no size-check row and no last-test row, both being on the chart; test progress shows in the header status); one chart below, with only its legend under it (no explanatory text).
- Chart: test wins (solid, with dots), each with its 9-test average (dotted) and 18-test average (wide, faint); prediction scores (dashed); the main network's step size as a red stepped line on a right-hand axis (0 to 0.001; ticks only at the values the step can take, 0.001 halved down to the floor, labelled where they do not crowd), drawn from when it was first recorded, with a hover line and a box listing every value. Clicking a legend label shows or hides that line (a wins line together with its two averages), remembered per AI. Periods of training against different opponents are shaded in different background colours.
- Tournament tab: pick players (the coded players, the ifelse AI, and each learning AI playing alone or inside the Benchmark search) and run round-robin games; results can be copied as text.
- Same moves (Tournament tab): two AIs play each other; in every position the AI not to move is asked what it would play; shown: the share of positions where both pick the same move.
- Decks tab (4 Nov 2026): the lab builds its own decks. Pool: 6 decks per faction, its premades (2 to 4) and random decks (10 cards of the faction x3) for the rest, at least 2 (8 Nov 2026). First round: every pool deck plays every other one ("games per pair", default 10, both seats, each pair of games dealt the same). Then, faction by faction, a new deck is made from one of the faction's unfinished decks, each in turn (the one with the fewest decks made from it first; 8 Nov 2026): one card's copies swapped, or one time in five one copy more or less (30-40 cards). Chosen from the card statistics like a player would: out goes the card that adds least (lowest win rate when played, less for cards played in under a quarter of their games); in comes first a card within 1 of its cost, preferring cards often played with the deck's 3 best cards, and a card counts 0.02 less for each other pool deck of the faction holding it; untried cards count as slightly good; a new deck that shares 70% or more of its cards (copies held by both, over the smaller deck) with another pool deck of its faction is not tried; random choices only before the first round has given statistics. The new deck plays every pool deck. If it is at least probably better than the deck it was made from (z of the win rates 1 or more), both play a fresh set of the same games (same opponents, seats and deals), and only if the new deck is clearly better there (z 2 or more) does it replace the deck it was made from (8 Nov 2026; before, it replaced the faction's weakest deck and every faction's pool became near-copies of its best deck). (Without the fresh games, 11% of decks got in by luck alone in a test with coin-flip results; with them 0.4%; in a test where one card truly helped, it spread to 30 of the 42 pool decks.) No deck is protected. A deck is finished when 30 decks made from it in a row failed; the search ends when every pool deck is finished. Each game is played by the same AI on both sides, the two chosen AIs taking turns. "Measure the premades": each premade (a copy outside the pool) plays every pool deck, as a yardstick. Card statistics per faction, from the deck search's games: games in a deck, share played when in the deck (marked "always played" at 90% or more, "rarely played" under 20%), win rate when played, win rate of its decks, the card most often played with it (more than chance), and "With / without": up to 3 pool decks holding the card play the pool once more with the card's copies replaced by a lab-only plain unit of its cost (size 1, no keywords; strength + defence = the design budget, as in design_gate.py). Export as a text file. Use in the game (8 Nov 2026): each pool deck has buttons "Deck 1" to "Deck 4" that make it the faction's premade deck of that number (3 and 4 are added next to the faction's built-in two), "Use the best four of each faction in the game" does that for every faction with its four best pool decks, "Use the built-in premades again" undoes it. The saved decks are kept in the browser (localStorage key threeBases.savedPremades, read by the game, the scenarios and the lab, so both pages must be opened from the same place); they replace the premade lists of decks-premade.js (a saved deck with a card no longer in the faction, or outside 30-40 cards, is skipped), the game's decks page shows them in place of the premades, and the game's computer opponents and every lab AI's training, tests, tournaments and tuning play them. The lab's workers get them with their program. The lab reloads after saving (whatever runs pauses; pool decks keep their own lists and results). Speed: a first round at 10 games per pair is 8610 games, about an hour on 2 cores with a learning AI playing alone.
- ifelse AI tab: tunes all the ifelse AI's values at once with CMA-ES against a mix of opponents ticked from: Benchmark and each learning AI in the lab (playing as in its tests: no look-ahead); at least one ticked; games are shared equally among them, 12 in a row each so that every block covers all matchups and both seats. Every value lives on one common scale (0 = its starting value): weights move by a factor e^x (a weight starting at 0 by 5·x), shares on the logit scale (×2), whole numbers by x times their range (rounded, within their limits), switches flip when x crosses ±0.25. Since 8 Nov 2026 it is the diagonal form (sep-CMA-ES): one learned step size per value, no combinations, its learning rates (n+2)/3 times the full form's (with 103 values the full form's were about 0.0001 per generation, so it never learned which values matter). Each generation, the candidates (18) are sampled around the centre in mirrored pairs (centre + step, centre - step); each plays "Games per candidate" games (default 300) on the same schedule; win rate with draws counting half; the centre moves toward the better half (standard CMA-ES weights and updates; step size capped at 1). When the step size falls below 0.02 it restarts (until 8 Nov 2026 also when the generation average hadn't improved by more than 1 point for 20 generations: that fired by luck and threw away the learned step sizes) around the current centre with twice the candidates (up to 40), marked on the chart. Changing the mix restarts only the generation under way (marked). Start, Pause/Resume, Start over (back to the starting values), "Export ifelse AI", its own "Games at once". One thing runs at a time: starting the tuning stops training and pauses a tournament, and starting either pauses the tuning. End of a generation (9 Nov 2026): results are kept per candidate and deal; once every game has started, half the cores or more are idle and the unfinished games lie in at most 5% of the deals (at least 1), the generation ends without those deals, dropped for every candidate alike, and the running games are stopped (their workers replaced). After a pause, games that were running are played again. The centre itself plays the same games each generation. After the centre moved, the next generation also grades the centre before the move on the same games; if that one won clearly more, the centre goes back to it and the step size shrinks by 10% (10 Nov 2026: clearly = its lead, game by game over the same games, is more than twice the luck in that lead (2 standard errors); before, any lead counted, which was luck about every second round and shrank the step for nothing) . Otherwise the centre always moves to the weighted mean of the better half (CMA-ES's own step). Rejected (9 Nov 2026): moving only when the better half's average grade beat the centre's; tries change every value at random and mostly score below the centre even when their mean is better, so the centre never moved in 13 rounds and only the step size shrank. The new centre is judged by itself, against the centre before it, in the next generation. Chart (9 Nov 2026): per generation one gold line, the centre's win rate (the ifelse AI as it is; axis "games won"); the candidates' average and the best candidate's are in the hover text of each generation, not drawn. A tuning from before 8 Nov 2026 keeps its centre and starts the new form from it (marked on the chart). Table: every value, starting value vs the centre (changes highlighted). The ifelse AI plays with the centre's values as a tournament player; "Export for analysis" includes its state.
- Values tuned (10 Nov 2026): 64 of the 83. Not tuned, kept at their start and marked "(not tuned)" in the table: the five "moves examined" values, the 7 switches (a flip is a jump, not a step) and the 7 goal-lane values (goal lanes start off, so they change nothing).
- Search depth while tuning: 1, "1, and the best 3 checked against the reply" (the default, 10 Nov 2026), 2 or 3. The default values every own move 1 move ahead, then checks only the best 3 against the opponent's best reply (depth 2 with 3 own moves examined). Why: at 1 ahead every move is judged as if the round ended right after it, and only the threat values stand in for the opponent's answer; it never looks at what the opponent can play from hand. Measured on 34 positions: 9 ms per move at 1 ahead, 27 ms with the second look, 50 ms at 2 ahead; the second look picked another move in 5 of the 34. A Benchmark opponent looks 2 ahead then.
- Aim of the ifelse tuning (Kaptain, 9 Nov 2026): an ifelse AI that plays with foresight at depth 1 (one step), so it plays fast and still sees what a deep search sees.
- Hidden tab (9 Nov 2026): while any games run in the lab, it holds a Web Lock (the browser does not freeze the tab) and plays a near-silent sound (browsers run tabs playing sound at full speed; the tab shows a speaker icon); both stop when no games run. The sound starts only after a click on the page (the Start buttons).
- Tuning by lessons removed (10 Nov 2026). Two grades were tried at 1 move ahead: agreeing with move values from 3 moves ahead (held every try back to the centre), then guessing who won from each board (Texel tuning). In Kaptain's run of 430 rounds the second raised the grade from 82% to about 86% in 50 rounds and then nothing, and games won against the opponent stayed around 32% (19% to 43%). Measured on 2178 boards: the starting values already grade 88.9%, a blind guess 75.8%, the grade jumps about 1.5 points between sets of 200 games, more than tries differ. Do not rebuild a grade that is not games won. A tuning from before starts over.
- Widths are settings, not tuned (9 Nov 2026): own moves 10, replies 10, the 3 best next moves (the third move examines at least 3); tuning never changes them (wider is always slower).
- Tuning (4 Nov 2026): starting values are the game's Benchmark values (BENCH_VALUES), plus the three values it has beyond the earlier list (first move: extra per worth of enemy units killed, passing right after the opponent passed, skipping free moves that don't help). Lab starting values that differ from the Benchmark (9 Nov 2026): 10 own moves and 10 replies examined, first moves picked by the round end played out (rootRound on). Keyword values: one per line of js/ai/keyword-worth.js whose keyword is in the cards (kw_<field>, start 0; see section 15); the effect values per kind of effect and timing (op_, on_) are gone (9 Nov 2026). Values are stored by name: when cards change and values come or go, the rest carry on (marked "values changed" on the chart) and new ones start at their start. Candidates per generation: CMA-ES's usual number for the count of values. Every candidate plays the same deals (game i of a generation is dealt the same for all). Deck pairs: each generation goes on through the matchup's pairs where the last one stopped (8 Nov 2026; before, every generation played the same first pairs, so with more premades some decks were never used). "Search depth while tuning" (1, 2 or 3; starts 1): tuning games use it for both the ifelse AI and the Benchmark (how far it looks is not a tuned value; widths of deeper moves keep their start). "Make this the Benchmark": the centre plays the game's Benchmark, both at the tuning depth, for the set number of games (default 200; seats and decks in turn, each pair of games dealt the same); only if it wins clearly (the lab's "clearly better") a file is downloaded, which tools/set_benchmark.py writes into the game. Tuning at depth 1 plays about 1 game a second per core.
- The earlier Benchmark's values (the start of the list, before the game's Benchmark values replace them): moves examined at each of the five levels (8, 6, 4, 4, 4); skip moves that change nothing (switch, on); worth of a base 220, a dot 45; with one base, per own dot in the other lanes 30; two bases +300, opponent's two bases -450; lane income 12 per supply, counted up to 6, share 0.3 in a won lane; unspent supply 3; carry-over 10; cards in hand 4 (own minus opponent's); supply cost of cards in hand 0; unit worth per supply 10, strength 2, defence 3, (supply + supply bonus) 5, front-row bonus 6; won lane: 2 front units in full, others 0.1; contested lane: each further unit 0.15 less, at least 0.3; threat multiplier 1, kill share 0.6, damage 2, side-to-move weights 1 / 0.3 (opponent to move) and 0.9 / 0.3 (own move); threat switches (all on): each attacker used once with supply shared and several attackers able to finish one target together, Bodyguards protect neighbours, units that attacked this round threaten nothing; out of supply with 0/1/2 units 1000/300/100; a draw -500; goal lanes (switch, off) with third-lane share 0.7, stick 1.5 and lane-need weights 2, 1.5, 0.15, 0.1, 0.2. Cards in the opponent's hand are covered by the search itself, which sees them.
- "Export for analysis" downloads everything measured (settings, every AI's record and tests, size checks, tournament) as one file to examine.
