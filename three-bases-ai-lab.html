ifelse AI

An AI that sees both hands and searches the real position, with no rules of thumb: every judgement is a weighted term. All its values (weights, how far and how wide it looks, switches) are tuned at once with CMA-ES against the opponents you tick, starting from the Benchmark's values. Each generation, several candidates around a centre (every value changed by a different amount, in pairs: one up, its twin down by the same amount) play the same set of games; the centre moves toward the better half, but only when that half beat the centre in the same games (else the centre stays and the steps get smaller), and the method learns how far to change each value. The centre itself, the ifelse AI as it is now, plays the same games too: the gold line is its win rate, the dashed line the candidates' average (lower, since most changes are tries that fail). It restarts with more candidates once its steps have become tiny. Draws count half.

Decks

The lab builds its own decks. A pool of 6 decks per faction (its premades, 2 to 4, and random decks of 10 cards ×3 for the rest, at least 2) first plays every deck against every other one. Then, faction by faction, it makes a new deck from one of the faction's unfinished decks (each in turn) by swapping one card's copies (or one copy more or less), chosen from the card statistics: out goes the card that adds least, in comes first a card of about the same cost, preferring cards often played with the deck's best cards. The new deck plays every pool deck and replaces the deck it was made from only if it scores clearly higher, so each faction keeps 6 separate lines of decks; a new deck that shares 70% or more of its cards with another pool deck of its faction is not tried. A deck is finished when 30 decks made from it in a row failed. Each game is played by the same AI on both sides, the two AIs taking turns, so decks are compared, not AIs; every game records which cards were played.

    Card statistics

    "Win rate when played" flatters cards that are played when a side is already winning, and "win rate of its decks" flatters cards that sit in strong decks; "With / without" is the fair measure: up to 3 pool decks holding the card play the pool once more with the card's copies replaced by a plain unit of its cost (the design budget's strength and defence, no keywords).
